### 1. The Golden Rule of Framing: Psychological Distance
Cỡ cảnh không phải là việc phóng to hay thu nhỏ màn hình đơn thuần. Cỡ cảnh là **Mức độ thân mật tâm lý (Psychological Intimacy)** giữa khán giả và nhân vật:
* **Càng ở xa nhân vật**: Khán giả phán xét bằng **Lý trí** (quan sát toàn cục thế giới, thấy con người nhỏ bé trước vũ trụ/số phận).
* **Càng lại gần nhân vật**: Khán giả cảm nhận bằng **Trái tim & Bản năng** (nhìn thấy nỗi đau, giọt nước mắt, nhịp thở dồn dập, sự đe dọa sinh tử).

### 2. The 3 Critical Confusion Pairs in Shot Scales (Cỡ Cảnh Dễ Nhầm Lẫn)

#### Pair 1: Extreme Long Shot (ELS) vs. Long Shot / Full Shot (LS / FS)
* **Extreme Long Shot (ELS / Cực Toàn)**: *Môi trường là nhân vật chính*. Con người chỉ là hạt bụi (< 5% khung hình). Cảm giác: Sự cô độc tột cùng, định mệnh nghiệt ngã, sự nhỏ bé của kiếp người trước vũ trụ (*Dune*, hoang mạc, tàn tích).
* **Long Shot / Full Shot (LS / FS / Toàn Cảnh)**: *Con người và môi trường ngang hàng*. Thấy trọn vẹn từ đỉnh đầu đến gót chân chạm đất. Cảm giác: Xem diễn biến một hành động trọn vẹn (bước xuống xe, ngã quỵ, khiêu vũ).

#### Pair 2: Cowboy Shot (MLS) vs. Medium Shot (MS)
* **Cowboy Shot (MLS / Trung Toàn Ngang Đùi)**: Cắt ở **giữa đùi**. Bộc lộ tư thế đứng hiếu chiến, dáng dấp sẵn sàng rút vũ khí hoặc lao vào ẩu đả, hai bàn tay lơ lửng gần thắt lưng.
* **Medium Shot (MS / Trung Cảnh Ngang Thắt Lưng)**: Cắt ở **thắt lưng / rốn**. Góc nhìn tự nhiên khi hai người đứng nói chuyện ngoài đời thực. Thể hiện cử chỉ thao tác tay bình thường, ngôn ngữ cơ thể thư thái.

#### Pair 3: Close-Up (CU) vs. Extreme Close-Up (ECU / Macro)
* **Close-Up (CU / Cận Cảnh Cằm - Trán)**: Lấy trọn khuôn mặt (từ hõm cổ lên đỉnh đầu). Thể hiện **tổng hòa cảm xúc**: nụ cười cay đắng, ánh mắt căm hờn, vẻ mặt tuyệt vọng. Hậu cảnh xóa phông hoàn toàn.
* **Extreme Close-Up (ECU / Macro / Đặc Tả Vi Mô)**: Chỉ lấy **một chi tiết sinh tử**: đồng tử co thắt, giọt mồ hôi rơi khỏi cằm, ngón tay run rẩy gạt kíp nổ, giọt máu nhỏ trên văn tự. Kể về **manh mối định đoạt** hoặc bản năng sinh tồn. Tuyệt đối cấm thoại ở cỡ này.

### 3. The 3 Critical Confusion Pairs in Camera Angles (Góc Quay Dễ Nhầm Lẫn)

#### Pair 1: Low-Angle (Góc Thấp) vs. High-Angle (Góc Cao)
* **Low-Angle (Góc Thấp hất lên 30°–45°)**: Đặt người xem vào vị thế một đứa trẻ ngước nhìn người lớn. Nhân vật trông đồ sộ, quyền lực, ngạo mạn hoặc vĩ đại (bạo chúa, người hùng thức tỉnh).
* **High-Angle (Góc Cao chúc xuống 30°–45°)**: Đặt người xem vào vị thế kẻ khổng lồ nhìn xuống đàn kiến. Nhân vật bị đè nén xuống sàn nhà, đầu to chân nhỏ, bất lực, cô độc (tù nhân bị thẩm vấn, kẻ thua trận).

#### Pair 2: Eye-Level vs. Dutch Angle (Góc Nghiêng Lệch 15°–25°)
* **Eye-Level (Góc Ngang Tầm Mắt 0°)**: Trạng thái bình thường, tỉnh táo, thực tế, hai người bình đẳng nhìn nhau.
* **Dutch Angle (Góc Nghiêng Lệch Trục)**: Não bộ bị kích thích báo động vì mất cân bằng đường chân trời. Báo hiệu: bất thường, nguy hiểm cận kề, say rượu, trúng độc, cơn điên loạn, phản bội.

#### Pair 3: Over-The-Shoulder (OTS) vs. First-Person P.O.V vs. SnorriCam (Body-Mount)
* **Over-The-Shoulder (OTS / Qua Vai)**: Khán giả đứng **sau lưng nhân vật A** để chứng kiến biểu cảm của B. Tạo chiều sâu 3D đối thoại và thế trận hai bên.
* **Point-Of-View (P.O.V / Mắt Nhân Vật)**: Khán giả **chính là nhân vật**, ống kính là mắt nhân vật. Bắt buộc 20% mép dưới có bàn tay/vũ khí (Định luật mỏ neo tiền cảnh).
* **SnorriCam (Body-Mounted Chest Rig)**: Máy gắn chặt vào ngực nhân vật chĩa ngược vào mặt. Gương mặt bất động ở tâm màn hình, toàn bộ thế giới phía sau quay cuồng dập dềnh (dành cho sốc thuốc, say xỉn, sang chấn tâm lý cực đoan).

### 4. The 5 Fatal Camera Movement Confusions in AI Prompting (Tử Huyệt Động Năng)

#### Confusion 1: ZOOM IN (Thu Phóng Quang Học) vs. DOLLY IN / PUSH-IN (Tịnh Tiến Vật Lý)
* **ZOOM IN (Quang học)**: Máy đứng yên, chỉ vặn tiêu cự ống kính. Hình ảnh bị phóng to phẳng lì (flatten), hậu cảnh bị kéo bẹp dí, triệt tiêu chiều sâu 3D (giống camera giám sát an ninh).
* **DOLLY IN / PUSH-IN (Tịnh tiến vật lý)**: Cả thân máy quay bước chân hoặc chạy ray tiến thẳng lại gần nhân vật. **Tạo hiện tượng Thị Sai (Parallax)**: Tiền cảnh trôi dạt nhanh ra ngoài viền, hậu cảnh mở rộng chiều sâu sống động 3 chiều. Áp lực tâm lý tăng lên chân thực.
* 👉 **Lệnh chuẩn AI**: Banned `zoom in`. Thay bằng: `slow physical eye-level dolly push-in with dynamic spatial parallax`.

#### Confusion 2: PAN (Ngoái Cổ Tại Chỗ) vs. TRUCK / CRAB (Đi Bộ Ngang)
* **PAN (Pan Left / Right)**: Đứng yên một chỗ ngoái cổ sang trái/phải. Vị trí tương quan giữa các vật thể không đổi nhiều.
* **TRUCK / CRAB (Truck Left / Right)**: Cả người và máy quay bước đi ngang. Các vật ở gần (hàng rào, cột đèn) lướt qua ống kính cực nhanh trong khi tòa nhà xa trôi chậm, tạo cảm giác điện ảnh khối 3D tráng lệ.

#### Confusion 3: TILT (Gật/Ngửa Đầu) vs. PEDESTAL (Nâng/Hạ Toàn Thân)
* **TILT (Tilt Up / Down)**: Chân máy đứng yên ở một độ cao cố định, chỉ ngửa đầu máy lên trời hoặc chúi xuống đất (đổi góc nhìn).
* **PEDESTAL (Pedestal Up / Down)**: Giữ nguyên góc nhìn ngang mắt 0°, nhưng nâng toàn bộ thân máy thẳng đứng từ tầng 1 lên tầng 2 (đổi độ cao tuyệt đối, không đổi góc nhìn).

#### Confusion 4: STEADICAM / GIMBAL vs. HANDHELD (Cầm Tay Rung Lắc)
* **Steadicam / Gimbal**: Lướt đi êm ru như bóng ma lơ lửng, không có chấn động bước chân. Dành cho sự huyền bí, sang trọng, định mệnh không thể cản phá.
* **Handheld**: Máy chao đảo theo nhịp thở và bước chân dồn dập. Dành cho bạo lực thực chiến, tài liệu, hoảng loạn, chiến tranh.

#### Confusion 5: VERTIGO DOLLY-ZOOM (Zolly / Trombone Effect)
* **Cơ chế**: Máy quay lùi xa (Dolly Out) đồng thời ống kính phóng tới (Zoom In) - hoặc ngược lại.
* **Ảo giác thị giác**: Khuôn mặt nhân vật giữ nguyên kích thước không đổi, nhưng toàn bộ hành lang hoặc căn phòng phía sau bị hút dãn dài ra thăm thẳm hoặc co sập lại.
* **Ý nghĩa**: Báo hiệu sự thật chấn động khiến thế giới nhân vật nứt toác và sụp đổ hoàn toàn.

## 🧭 § 0. DIMENSION 1: SCENE SEGMENTATION STRUCTURE (SINGLE-SHOT VS. MULTI-SHOT TAXONOMY)

Before specifying shot scales or camera angles, the director must select the macro segmentation track:

```
                                      MACRO SCENE SEGMENTATION FORK
                                                    │
                 ┌──────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────┐
                 ▼                                                                     ▼
     [TRACK A: SINGLE-SHOT CONTINUOUS TAKE]                             [TRACK B: MULTI-SHOT CUT SEQUENCE]
     • 1 SINGLE CAMERA RECORDING CONTINUOUSLY (0 Cut)                   • MULTIPLE CAMERA SETUPS EDITED VIA HARD CUTS (2–4 Cuts)
     • Spatiotemporal preservation & accumulative pressure              • Adversarial dialectic & cognitive shock
     • Fluid in-camera re-framing (180° Orbit, Crane, Push-in)          • Shot-Reverse-Shot locked to the 180° Action Axis
     • Single speaker OR Dynamic 2-Speaker Dialogue (timed: 0b §2–3)    • Rapid ping-pong verbal sparring (timed: 0b §7)    
     • Natural Dynamic Deceleration (Zero frozen frames)        • Clean Editorial Cut or Action Continuation
     • Best: Inner V.O., Solitary, Orbit Dialogue, Kinetic Momentum     • Best: Courtroom sparring, Truth bombs, Mob clash
```

> 💡 **THE FOUNDATIONAL INTUITION & THE 3 UNIVERSAL CONTROL KNOBS (BẢN CHẤT CỐT LÕI & BỘ 3 CÔNG CỤ ĐIỀU KHIỂN)**:
> • **The Hardware Fork (Phần Cứng)**:
>   - **Single-Shot (Track A) = 1 Single Camera Roll**: 1 camera records continuously from 00:00 to 00:10.0 without pausing, stopping, or cutting. The camera can be static or move as a living 3D dancer across the physical set.
>   - **Multi-Shot (Track B) = Multiple Camera Setups Edited via Hard Cuts**: Multiple distinct camera angles (e.g. Cam A on Speaker, Cam B on Listener, Cam C on Prop) spliced together via deliberate editorial cuts (`2 to 4 Cuts`) along the 180° Action Axis.
> • **The 3 Universal Control Knobs (Bộ 3 Công Cụ Điều Khiển Cảm Xúc)**:
>   Whether operating 1 camera (Single-Shot) or multiple cameras (Multi-Shot), the director manipulates 3 universal parameters:
>   1. **Framing & Shot Scale (Cỡ Cảnh)**: *"Where to place the camera in distance?"* (Far = Rational context & world; Close = Biological instinct, tears & truth).
>   2. **Angle & Axis (Góc Quay)**: *"How to tilt, pitch, and yaw the lens?"* (Low-Angle = Dominance & power; High-Angle = Vulnerability & oppression; Dutch = Madness & betrayal).

> 👁️ **THE BLINKING EYE LAW (ẨN DỤ CHỚP MẮT ĐỂ KHÔNG BAO GIỜ NHẦM LẪN)**:  
> • **Single-Shot (Cú máy đơn)**: Like keeping your **eyes wide open without blinking for a single millisecond (0 Cuts)**. Designed for rapid ping-pong clash, adversarial friction, and cognitive shocks.

### 🅰️ TRACK A: SINGLE-SHOT CONTINUOUS TAKE (Unbroken Cinematic Take / 0 Cut)
* **Essence**: 1 continuous, unbroken camera trajectory throughout the entire 10 seconds. Preserves spatial and temporal integrity with strictly zero edit cuts.
* **Core Philosophy**: *"Spatiotemporal Preservation & Accumulative Pressure (Bảo Toàn Không-Thời Gian & Tích Tụ Áp Suất)"*.
* **AI Diffusion Advantage**: Maximum spatiotemporal stability (95%+ success rate). Zero facial morphing, zero costume drift, zero background latent regeneration.
* **The 5 Core Application Archetypes**:
  1. **Inner Monologue & Contemplation (Model 4 V.O.)**: Character lost in contemplation, voiceover guiding thoughts (timed by grammar §2).
  2. **Solitary Pressurized Scene (Model 3)**: Lone character confronting grief, shame, prayer, or realization.
  3. **Continuous Kinetic Physics & High-Speed Trajectories (kinetic take, usually wordless)**: Tracking subject plummeting, sprinting through a collapsing corridor, or vaulting over thresholds where cuts would break momentum ($V_{cam} = V_{subj}$).
  4. **Tactile Micro-Mechanics & Delicate Prop Interaction**: Hand breaking a wax seal, slipping poison into wine, drawing a concealed blade. Keeps prop geometry 100% stable.
  5. **Dynamic In-Camera Multi-Angle 2-Person Dialogue (Two-Shot Choreography)**:
     - **Mechanism**: The camera moves continuously through multiple angles/setups within 0 cuts (e.g. Low-Angle OTS on Speaker A $	o$ 180° Orbital Arc $	o$ Frontal Eye-Level on Speaker B).
     - **Dialogue Budget**: timed in seconds by `0b-temporal-camera-grammar.md` §2–§3; lines land before an arc starts or after it settles (§5). Best in 15 s clips when both lines are full.
     - **Non-Overlapping Dialogue Windows**: Spoken turns are distributed dynamically (e.g. Phase 1 Speaker A speaks while Speaker B reacts silently $\to$ Phase 2 Speaker B counters while Speaker A absorbs impact), with unbroken kinetic and emotional momentum flowing naturally across the full 10.0s take. Strictly BAN rigid cutoff dogma (e.g. claiming dialogue cannot occur after 00:08.0 or 00:08.5).
* **Terminal Kinetic Flow & Biological Settle**: Camera decelerates smoothly along an exponential deceleration curve, settling into a stable resting position with continuous biological micro-motion (breathing, drifting dust, flickering light) from the terminal seconds right up to 00:10.0, strictly zero frozen frames.
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibitions**:
  - **NEVER use static talking-head two-shots with overlapping dialogue**. Without dynamic camera choreography and separated temporal windows, simultaneous speech in Single-Shot triggers fatal AI lip-sync drift and facial morphing.

> 🎬 **MASTER DIRECTORIAL ESSENCE**: *"When acting reaches its highest peak of truth, the camera must stop moving."* (David Fincher, Ingmar Bergman).  
> In dramatic storytelling, moving the camera during an actor's emotional breakdown is an amateur mistake that dilutes audience empathy. A **Locked-Off Performance Take (Track A-Locked)** physically anchors the camera on a stationary tripod, transferring 100% of the kinetic energy to the actor's internal somatic performance and environmental physics.

* **Core Applications**:
  1. **Emotional Peak & Breakdown**: Unspoken agony, weeping, shock, where any camera motion feels artificial.
  2. **Razor Monologue / Intimate Confession**: A single character delivering an unhurried, piercing line (8–14 words) directly or semi-profile, with zero camera distraction.
  3. **The Replace Silence (Model 0)**: 10 seconds of wordless realization, where the body's involuntary biological betrayals tell the whole story.
  4. **Inner Monologue (Model 4 V.O.)**: The character's face is locked in contemplation while the internal voice echoes.

* **The 5-Pillar Dedicated Prompt Optimization Protocol (Bảo Hiểm Chống Tượng Sáp / Wax Mannequin Trap)**:
  In generative AI video models (Runway, Kling, Sora, Wan 2.1, Hailuo), writing simply `"static camera"` frequently triggers a fatal failure mode: the model interprets the scene as a still photograph, freezing the actor into an emotionless wax statue. To unlock hyper-realistic performance on a locked camera, prompts MUST enforce:
  1. **Optical Anchor Rigidity**: Explicitly declare `locked-off eye-level static shot on a stationary tripod, completely fixed camera position with zero camera movement, zero panning, zero tracking, zero zoom.`
  2. **Hyper-Dense Somatic Micro-Acting Directing**: Replace camera movement vectors with continuous biological micro-actions distributed across time brackets:
     - `[00:00–00:03.5]`: Initial physical tension (rapid shallow chest heaving, tight jaw, masseter muscle twitching, eyes locked down).
     - `[00:03.5–00:07.0]`: Somatic rupture or spoken delivery (eyes welling with unshed tears, throat visibly swallowing down a choke, knuckles whitening against table).
     - `[00:07.0–00:10.0]`: Emotional aftermath (chin trembling, single tear spilling down cheek, eyelids fluttering half-closed as shoulders slowly slump).
  3. **Living Environmental Micro-Dynamics (Anti-Freeze Insurance)**: Mandate continuous background/environmental kinetic elements to keep the DiT diffusion engine actively rendering:
     - `Microscopic dust motes floating and dancing lazily through the diagonal morning sunbeam`.
     - `A delicate curl of steam rising steadily from a teacup on the table`.
     - `Loose wisps of hair fluttering gently against the temple in a subtle domestic draft`.
  4. **Tactile Prop Friction & Internal Staging**: The character MUST maintain tactile contact with an architectural fixture or prop (clutching bedframe, pressing thumb against bandaged wrist, gripping wooden armrest). Movement is internal (leaning forward 2 inches, head lowering, gaze sweeping upward).
  5. **Clean Single-Subject Frame**: Single-subject framing (CU, MCU, or Dirty Single). Background characters, if present, are soft-blurred and purely reactive.

When power or attention shifts inside the take, master directors (Cuarón, Deakins, Fincher) let the camera travel through **2 to 3 distinct perspectives in one take with zero cuts**, each move with a motive (Doctrine §2.3) and a duration from `0b-temporal-camera-grammar.md` §5.

### 1. Archetype 1: The 180° Orbital Arc Dialogue (Chuyển Góc Đối Thoại Bằng Cung Tròn 180°)
* **Cinematic Mechanics**: The camera begins in an Over-the-Shoulder (OTS) position behind Character A looking at Character B. Character B delivers Line 1 while the camera holds the OTS. The camera then executes a continuous, motivated semicircular glide around the central spatial anchor (e.g. table, brazier, console), sweeping 180 degrees (≥ 5 s, grammar §5) to settle directly in front of Character A, and only then does Character A counter with Line 2. No lip-synced line is spoken while the arc travels.
* **Audience Impact**: The viewer experiences the narrative depth and psychological coverage of two separate coverage setups (Shot/Reverse-Shot) without a single disruptive edit cut, maintaining 100% spatiotemporal immersion and continuous 3D parallax.
* **AI Video Safety**: By maintaining a static physical anchor in the rotational center (PROP-01), generative diffusion models preserve exact 3D spatial coordinates without warping background geometry.

### 2. Archetype 2: The Vertical Elevation Power Shift (Chuyển Giao Quyền Lực Bằng Cao Độ Trục Tung)
* **Cinematic Mechanics**: Starts at a Low-Angle (30° pitch up) framing a menacing standing figure who issues an ultimatum. As the dialogue finishes, the camera floats upward along a smooth Pedestal/Jib vertical vector, rotating down into a suffocating High-Angle (45° pitch down) looking directly onto the seated listener absorbing the moral blow.
* **Audience Impact**: Visually depicts the shifting equilibrium of psychological power within a continuous, unbroken movement.

### 3. Archetype 3: The Subtext & Living Reality Mandate (Chống Rập Khuôn & Giáo Điều)
* **Action Anchoring**: Characters must NEVER stand frozen like theatrical mannequins reciting lines into thin air. Every line of dialogue must be anchored to a mundane or urgent **diegetic physical action** (adjusting a steering wheel, peeling open a coffee cup, snapping on gloves, cocking a weapon, wiping rainwater).
* **Subtext over Exposition**: Spoken words must layer beneath the literal text. Characters speak of trivial logistics, weather, or tactical choices while their micro-biomechanics and eye contact convey life-and-death moral betrayal.

### 4. Archetype 4: The Dynamic In-Camera Transition & Lip-Sync Visibility Mandate (Chuyển Góc Động Từ P.O.V Sang Medium Để Bắt Khẩu Hình)
* **The Invisibility Paradox in Downward P.O.V.**:
  - In a Subjective First-Person P.O.V. looking downward (e.g., at hands, a dropped weapon, a cold meal, or evidence), **THE CHARACTER'S LIPS AND FACIAL FEATURES ARE INVISIBLE TO THE CAMERA**.
  - Directorial Rule: A character whose eyes the camera represents **CANNOT deliver dialogue with visible lip-sync while the camera remains locked in downward P.O.V.** Attempting to force lip-sync during downward P.O.V. confuses AI diffusion models, triggering phantom-face hallucinations and bizarre limb mutations.
  - While in downward P.O.V., only tactile interactions, physical actions, and biological acoustics (choking sobs, ragged gasps, trembling sighs, diegetic acoustic clatters) are permitted.
* **The Motivated Transition to Frontal Medium Close-Up (MCU)**:
  - When the protagonist speaks, the camera must **dynamically transition**:
    - **Single-Shot (Track A)**: Smooth pedestal lift and 90°–180° lateral orbital arc glide, flowing seamlessly from downward P.O.V. into an objective **Eye-Level Medium Close-Up (MCU) / Medium Shot (MS)** framing the speaker's face, capturing glistening tears, facial micro-tensions, and authentic lip-sync!
    - **Multi-Shot (Track B)**: Hard editorial cut (`[00:00–00:03.0] Downward P.O.V. insert …` ➔ `[00:03.0–00:07.0] Cut to a frontal eye-level medium close-up …`) along the 180° Action Axis.
* **Opposing Speaker Exception**: If an opposing character is standing directly before the P.O.V. lens (Direct Fourth-Wall Contact), that opposing character CAN speak with full synchronous lip-sync because their face is completely visible within the frame.

### 5. Multi-Timeline Rhythm Shapes (10s, 15s, 30s) — Illustrative
> Duration modes, phase counts, and the 15 s / 30 s shapes live in `0b-temporal-camera-grammar.md` §6 and §9. The shapes below are examples, not templates: split points come from the beats.
* **Motivated Camera Law**: a static frame with living subjects is valid; move the camera only when attention, information, or the inner state moves (Doctrine §2.3).
  - **10 s (default)**, e.g. `[00:00–00:03.5]` trigger or tactile anchor → `[00:03.5–00:07.0]` frontal framing for the spoken climax → `[00:07.0–00:10.0]` counter-reaction and living settle.
  - **15 s (only on user request)**, e.g. `[00:00–00:04.0]` context → `[00:04.0–00:09.0]` main exchange → `[00:09.0–00:12.5]` the turn lands → `[00:12.5–00:15.0]` settle.
  - **30 s (only on user request; chained 3 × 10 s if the user asks)**: Act I `[00:00–00:08]` arrival and trigger → Act II `[00:08–00:22]` 2–3 exchanges with at least one power shift → Act III `[00:22–00:30]` reveal or decision, then a living settle ≥ 2 s.

### 🏃 The One-Move Rule (Anti-Compounded Action Law)
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **ONE PRIMARY KINETIC MOMENTUM + ONE BIOLOGICAL REACTION PER 3–4S SHOT**:
> In generative AI video, compounding multiple independent actions into a single 3–4 second shot window ("vừa A vừa B vừa C") overloads the motion latent space, resulting in severe AI limb morphing, phantom hands, and motion distortion.
> - **The Rule**: In each shot, a character executes strictly **ONE primary physical move** (e.g., turning a bolt, taking two steps forward, reaching for a hand) + **ONE biological settling reaction** (e.g., a sharp exhalation, a tightening jaw, an eye lock).
> - Chaining sequential multi-tasking is strictly forbidden within a single shot window.

### 🅱️ TRACK B: MULTI-SHOT CUT SEQUENCE (Kinetic Montage / 2–4 Cuts)
* **Essence**: Editorial cutting between matching angles locked strictly to the **180° Action Axis** (Shot-Reverse-Shot OTS + Snap-Cut Choker at final second).
* **Core Philosophy**: *"Adversarial Dialectics & Cognitive Shock (Biện Chứng Đối Kháng & Cú Sốc Nhận Thức)"*.
* **AI Video Advantage**: Sharp dramatic friction, high narrative velocity, classic Hollywood shot/reverse-shot confrontation, maximum viewer retention.
* **The 5 Core Application Archetypes**:
  1. **Adversarial Verbal Sparring & Ping-Pong Clash (Model 1, Model 1B, Model 2)**:
     - Two opposing wills collide face-to-face. Words act as kinetic blades parried and returned across alternating cuts (density by seconds, grammar §7).
  2. **The Truth Bomb & Mask Shattering / Anagnorisis (Cú Lật Mặt Nạ)**:
     - Shot 1 (Antagonist gloating in Medium) $	o$ Shot 2 (Tight Insert on physical proof/warrant) $	o$ Shot 3 (Macro Choker on Antagonist's trembling pupil and collapsing arrogance).
  3. **Protagonist vs. Greek Chorus / Living Society (Cá Nhân Đối Đầu Đám Đông)**:
     - High-contrast scale collision: Shot 1 (Extreme Wide of roaring mob) $	o$ Shot 2 (Low-Angle MCU Hero speaking truth) $	o$ Shot 3 (Wide Mob silenced in collective breathless shock).
  4. **Cause-and-Effect Spatial Dispersion (Nhân - Quả Phân Tán)**:
     - Shot 1 (Trigger action at Frame-Left) $	o$ Shot 2 (Impact event detonating at Frame-Right) $	o$ Shot 3 (Reaction standoff).
  5. **Third-Party Moral Witnessing (Nhân Chứng Đạo Đức Thứ Ba)**:
     - The moral blow of a violent clash lands hardest not on the two combatants, but on an innocent third party watching from the sidelines (e.g. child, mother, loyal lieutenant).
* **Terminal Kinetic Flow & Biological Settle**: Decisive hard editorial cut to a tight Choker or Macro reaction shot, settling into a stable resting frame with continuous biological micro-motion (breathing, drifting dust) from the terminal seconds right up to 00:10.0, strictly zero frozen frames.
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibitions**:
  - **NEVER cross the 180° Action Axis** between cuts (reverses screen left/right gaze direction and confuses spatial orientation).
  - **NEVER cut between two identical shot sizes of the same character** without at least a 30-degree angle change (causes jarring jump cuts).
  - **NEVER allow dialogue to bleed into scene boundary**: Spoken words must naturally conclude in time for facial expressions and camera trajectory to resolve organically to 00:10.0.

### 🔀 TRACK AB: HYBRID CONTINUOUS TAKE + CUT (Hybrid Take + Reverse/Landing)
* **Essence**: A hybrid sequence that starts with one continuous, unbroken take of $\ge 5.0$ seconds (holding 2+ phases) to build accumulative dramatic pressure, followed by 1–2 editorial cuts along the 180° Action Axis to reveal the emotional shockwave or physical consequence.
* **Selection & Grammar**: Governed by [`0b-temporal-camera-grammar.md` §6.1, §8, §12](file:///d:/Project/Sven/engine/references/0b-temporal-camera-grammar.md).
* **Phasing Syntax**:
  - The opening take states its camera trajectory (e.g. `[00:00–00:05.5] Medium close-up on Duyen, slow push-in...`).
  - Phased extensions within the take use `In the same take — ...`.
  - Cuts start explicitly with `Cut to ...` on the 180° axis (e.g. `[00:05.5–00:10.0] Cut to a reverse close-up on Khang, eye level, static camera...`).
* **Best Archetype**: Confessions, confrontation climaxes, and moral verdicts where pressure must build in one unbroken breath, but the landing must be witnessed on the opposing face or the physical prop.

### 1. Third-Person Objective (Góc Nhìn Khách Quan Truyền Thống)
* **Definition**: The camera represents an invisible, objective cinematic observer in the physical room.
* **Directorial Function**: Impartial storytelling; allows audience to judge both sides of the moral conflict equally. Standard baseline for high-budget theatrical drama.

### 2. Subjective First-Person P.O.V. (Góc Nhìn Qua Mắt Nhân Vật)
* **Definition**: The camera lens **IS THE ACTUAL EYES** of the protagonist or combatant.
* **The Mandatory Foreground Anchor Law (Định Luật Mỏ Neo Tiền Cảnh)**:
  - Without an in-frame physical anchor, AI models hallucinate a disembodied drifting camera.
  - **Mandatory**: Lower 20-25% of frame MUST contain the protagonist's hands, sleeves, gloves, drawn weapon, or held prop (`protagonist's trembling scarred hands enter bottom-frame gripping the bronze ledger...`).
  - The opponent in front looks directly into the optical lens center (**Direct Fourth-Wall Eye Contact**).
* **The 4 Dynamic Variants of P.O.V.**:
  1. *The Scanning Tilt P.O.V. (P.O.V Đo Đạc)*: Eyes look down at held prop in foreground (Insert Shot) $\to$ Vertical Tilt-Up to Low-Angle Close-Up of menacing enemy.
  2. *The Saccadic Pan P.O.V. (P.O.V Tìm Kiếm Hoảng Loạn)*: Rapid eye saccades (micro whip-pans) darting from ransacked desk to empty safe.
  3. *The Frozen Retinal P.O.V. (P.O.V Sốc Đơ Cứng)*: Dead static lock staring at cornering antagonist who looks dead-center into lens.
  4. *The Running Head-Bob P.O.V. (P.O.V Chạy Trốn Nhấp Nhô)*: Organic rhythmic vertical bobbing and rotational yaw matching running footsteps.

* ⚡ **The Dynamic Perspective Transition Law (Định Luật Chuyển Góc Động Thoát Khỏi P.O.V — Anti-Static P.O.V Trap)**:
  - **The Visibility Imperative**: When the camera is in Subjective P.O.V. looking downward, the protagonist's face and mouth are completely outside the frame. The protagonist **CANNOT deliver spoken dialogue with on-screen lip-sync** during this phase (only physical actions, diegetic Foley, and biological sobs/breaths).
  - **Motivated In-Camera Transition**: To deliver spoken dialogue, the camera must dynamically transition out of P.O.V.:
    * *Track A (Single-Shot Continuous Take)*: A fluid pedestal lift and 90°–180° lateral orbital arc glide moving seamlessly from P.O.V. into an objective **Eye-Level Medium Close-Up (MCU) / Medium Shot (MS)** framing the speaker's face.
    * *Track B (Multi-Camera Cut Sequence)*: A hard editorial cut (`[00:00–00:03.5] Downward P.O.V. insert …` ➔ `[00:03.5–00:07.0] Cut to a frontal eye-level medium close-up …`) along the 180° Action Axis.
  - **Opposing Character Speech in P.O.V.**: An opposing character facing the lens CAN speak down the optical axis (**Direct Fourth-Wall Contact**).

### 3. Direct-to-Camera Vlog & Whistleblower (Góc Tự Sự Trực Diện / Selfie)
* **Definition**: The character holds a recording device (smartphone, handheld camera) or places it on a surface and **SPEAKS DIRECTLY TO THE AUDIENCE (Direct Address)**.
* **The Wide-Angle & Extended Arm Law (Định Luật Góc Rộng & Cánh Tay Vươn Dài)**:
  - Must specify wide focal length (**wide, arm's-length selfie perspective**) for authentic selfie perspective.
  - One arm extends toward edge of frame (`right arm extended off-screen holding the recording device at arm's length`).
* **The 4 Non-Negotiable Vlog Laws**:
  1. ⚡ *The Ghost Camera Rule (Không vẽ thiết bị)*: NEVER describe the phone/camera as an in-frame prop! The prompt text represents the video feed recorded by the device.
  2. ⚡ *The Continuous One-Hand Anchor (1 tay luôn giữ máy)*: One hand permanently holds device just out of frame; free hand gestures or interacts.
  3. ⚡ *The Anti-Filter Safety Protocol (Né nhạy cảm / né sexy)*: For female leads, STRICTLY BAN sensual words (`skin`, `porcelain`, `slender silhouette`, `curvaceous`, `lips parting`). Use dignified terms: `dignified poise`, `pageant-queen elegance`, `symmetrical facial harmony`, `warm communicative eyes`, `articulate speech cadence`.
  4. ⚡ *The Clean UI Negative Guard*: Negative constraint MUST purge device graphics: `Do not render visible phone, camera device, UI overlays, battery icons, red REC dots, timestamps, subtitles, or dialogue text.`
* **The 4 Dynamic Variants of Vlog**:
  1. *The Walking Whistleblower*: Medium Close-Up wide angle, walking through night street/rain, organic handheld micro-shake, streetlights raking face.
  2. *The Desktop Interrogation Vlog*: Device locked on dashboard/desk (Locked-off Tripod), subject sitting back, raising physical evidence into frame.
  3. *The Whip-Pan Flip Vlog*: Direct address on face $\to$ Rapid 180° whip-pan down to table or out window revealing crime scene.
  4. *The Signal Jam Cutoff*: Whispered truth close to lens before camera violently drops to floor, creating high-tension cliffhanger.

### 4. SnorriCam Body-Mounted Rig (Góc Gương Mặt Bất Động / Khủng Hoảng Tâm Lý Cực Đoan)
* **Definition**: A specialized mechanical body-rig (chest harness) is strapped to the actor, holding the camera on an extended boom pole 60cm–80cm away, pointing **DIRECTLY BACK AT THE ACTOR'S FACE**.
* **Visual Phenomenon**: The actor's face remains **PERFECTLY STABILIZED DEAD-CENTER IN THE FRAME**, while the background (walls, streets, lights) violently shakes, warps, retreats, and streaks past in extreme motion blur.
* **Directorial Function**: Conveys extreme dissociation, drug-induced euphoria/paranoia (*Requiem for a Dream*), intoxicated vertigo (*Mean Streets*), visceral panic, or existential shock where the human mind freezes while the physical world collapses around them.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `Snorricam body-mounted chest-rig shot. The camera is locked tightly facing [Subject]'s pale, hyperventilating face, keeping their head perfectly stabilized dead-center in the frame. As [Subject] stumbles through the neon-lit corridor, the walls and lights behind them violently shake and streak past in disorienting motion blur, capturing dilated pupils and visceral terror.`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Never use SnorriCam for calm, rational dialogue; it is exclusively reserved for peak somatic and psychological crises.

## 📸 § 1. DIMENSION 3: THE 14 CLASSICAL SHOT SCALES (BỘ 14 CỠ CẢNH ĐIỆN ẢNH)

Every shot scale represents a precise **psychological distance** between the character's soul and the viewer's eye:

```
[MACRO WORLD]                                                                                    [MICRO SOUL]
  EWS ──► WS ──► Solitary Env ──► Full ──► Cowboy ──► M2S ──► MS ──► OTS ──► MCU ──► CU ──► Choker ──► Head-On ──► ECU ──► Macro Insert
 (Cosmic)  (Room)      (Isolation)     (Body)   (Action)   (Standoff)  (Prop)   (Depth)  (Dialogue) (Emotion) (Trapped)  (Direct)   (Truth)    (Evidence)

### 1.0. The Framing Decision Doctrine — Ask Before You Choose

The matrix below is a **vocabulary, not a lookup table**. A shot scale is never picked from a beat number; it is chosen by answering three questions about the specific moment, in this order:

1. **What must the audience see right now?** The single piece of information the moment depends on — a face, a hand, the gap between two bodies, or the room itself.
2. **How close should the audience stand to this character, emotionally, at this instant?** Judging from a distance (reason) or feeling from inside (instinct) — see the Psychological Intimacy spectrum in the Golden Triangle section above.
3. **What changed since the previous frame?** A new scale must be earned by a shift: new information, a power reversal, an inner break. If nothing changed, the scale should not change either.

**Rules of use:**
- The **Typical Uses** column lists where a scale commonly serves well. It never assigns a scale to a beat. A Beat 4 climax may land hardest in a Solitary Environmental wide rather than an ECU.
- **Hard constraints stay hard; everything else is a choice.** Non-negotiable: dialogue safety (no visible lip-sync in ECU, Macro Insert, or downward P.O.V.), room-size physics (no Extreme Wide inside a 15m² room), and the 30° rule for same-scale cuts on one character.
- **Name the scale in prose inside prompts** ("a frontal medium close-up of…"), never as a bracket label or pipe-separated header.
- **Anti-formula self-check:** if the same scale progression (e.g., Insert → MCU → Two-Shot) repeats across three consecutive scenes, it has become a template. Rethink at least one of them from question 1.
### 1.0b. The Subject-to-Scale Invariance & Frame Capacity Law (Định Luật Sức Chứa Khung Hình & Triệt Tiêu Xung Đột Quang Học)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SUBJECT-TO-SCALE INVARIANCE IS AN ABSOLUTE OPTICAL PHYSICAL LAW IN AI GENERATIVE VIDEO.**
> Generative video diffusion models (Kling, Runway, Sora, Wan 2.1) do not have human intuition. They bind subject tokens directly to pixel bounding boxes calculated from the camera shot scale:
> 
> 1. **Single-Subject Scales (`MCU`, `CU`, `Choker`, `Head-On`, `ECU`)**:
>    - **Subject Capacity**: Strictly **1 Human Subject**.
>    - **Optical Reality**: Camera cuts tightly at mid-chest, neck, or chin. Shallow depth of field blurs everything behind the subject into soft bokeh.
>    - **FATAL ERROR TO AVOID**: NEVER describe the physical actions, trembling, eye movements, or expressions of a secondary character who is "two paces behind" or "in the background" inside a single-person Close-Up or Medium Close-Up!
>    - *Why this causes failure*: The diffusion model attempts to squeeze both faces into a single portrait crop, resulting in **grotesque facial morphing, phantom double heads, or warped floating limbs**.
>    - *Correct Directorial Handling*: Secondary characters are spatially **Off-Screen (`O.S.`)** in this shot. Direct ONLY the primary framed character.
> 
> 2. **Two-Subject Scales (`Medium Two-Shot [M2S]`, `Cowboy 2S`, `Two-Shot Profile`, `Over-the-Shoulder [OTS]`)**:
>    - **Subject Capacity**: Strictly **2 Interacting Subjects**.
>    - *M2S / Cowboy 2S*: Both characters are framed waist-up or mid-thigh-up, explicitly anchored at **Frame-Left** and **Frame-Right** across a physical tension gap. This is the ONLY shot scale where both characters can be directed reacting simultaneously in the same frame!
>    - *OTS*: 1 character's shoulder/nape sits blurred in the foreground outer 1/3 (spatial anchor, never looking into lens); the opposing character occupies the central 2/3 in razor-sharp focus.
> 
> 3. **Multi-Subject Scales (`Three-Shot`, `Group Master`, `Establishing Wide [WS]`)**:
>    - **Subject Capacity**: 3+ Characters.
>    - Requires triangular staging and 3 distinct planes of depth: Foreground (FG), Middleground (MG), Background (BG).
> 
> 4. **Non-Human Scales (`Macro Insert`, `ECU`)**:
>    - Strictly dedicated to inanimate props or isolated micro-anatomy (pupil, scar). Strictly ZERO facial acting or spoken dialogue.

### 1.2. Deep Dive: Specifications of the 14 Shot Scales

#### 1. Extreme Wide Shot (EWS / ELS / Đại Cảnh Định Mệnh)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Human subject occupies less than 10% of the screen height. Monumental landscape or architectural ruins dominate 90%+ of the frame.
* **Optical Depth**: Deep infinite focus; razor-sharp detail from foreground gravel to distant skyline.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Ultra-wide panoramic perspective (`deep infinite panoramic perspective`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `1/10` (Detached cosmic omniscience) or `10/10` (Overwhelming, crushing isolation).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 2 (World Staging)**, **Beat 6 (Cosmic Resolution & Maxim Hold)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ⚠️ *Restricted*. Voiceover (Model 4) only; facial pixels cannot resolve speech.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:07–00:10] Extreme Wide Shot, deep infinite focus, Static locked crane view capturing the tiny silhouette of [Subject] standing alone beneath towering cathedral arches as cold grey storm mist sweeps across the empty flagstone plaza...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: NEVER describe facial micro-biomechanics (tears, pupil dilation) in an EWS; models cannot render micro-expressions on a 20-pixel silhouette.

#### 2. Establishing Wide Shot (WS / Toàn Cảnh Thiết Lập Không Gian)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Shows the full architectural arena (courtroom, throne room, tavern, alley) with all active characters visible in their relative spatial coordinates.
* **Optical Depth**: Deep architectural focus; all key environmental landmarks (exits, hearths, tables) sharp and grounded.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Wide-angle environmental perspective (`wide architectural perspective, crisp spatial geometry`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `3/10` to `5/10` (Spatial anticipation).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 1 (Arena Setup)**, **Beat 3.1 (Arena Entry / Assembly)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ⚠️ *Moderate*. Permitted for loud proclamations or herald announcements.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:00–00:03] Wide Establishing Shot, deep architectural focus, Static eye-level framing capturing the vaulted council chamber, the massive long oak table positioned dead-center between opposing rows of iron-clad sentries...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Do not crowd foreground with random clutter; preserve clean negative space to establish clear movement vectors.

#### 3. Solitary Environmental Shot (Cảnh Cô Độc Nuốt Chửng)
* **Framing Boundaries**: A single character isolated within an unnaturally vast, cavernous, or ruined room (e.g. seated alone at the far end of an immense council table).
* **Optical Depth**: Cavernous falloff; deep atmospheric shadows swallowing background walls.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Wide-to-normal environmental lens with moody light falloff (`cavernous spatial depth, deep atmospheric falloff`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `8/10` (Unbearable grief, abandonment, guilt).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 4.3 (All Hope Is Lost)**, **Beat 5 (Grief & Acceptance)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ✅ *100% Safe with Model 4 V.O.* or silent heaving breath.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:00–00:04] Solitary Environmental Shot, deep cavernous shadow falloff, Slow backward dolly glide capturing [Subject] sitting alone at the edge of the forty-foot mahogany banquet table surrounded by towering empty high-back chairs...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Do not include lively background characters or distracting props; the subject must be completely, agonizingly alone.

#### 4. Full Shot (FS / Toàn Cảnh Cơ Thể Toàn Diện)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Full human figure from the soles of the boots to the crown of the head, with the ground plane explicitly anchored beneath feet.
* **Optical Depth**: Balanced environmental depth; subject fully sharp while distant background softens slightly.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Standard natural perspective lens (`balanced full-body depth of field`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `4/10` to `7/10` (Physical presence & bearing).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 1 (Entrance of Champion/Tyrant)**, **Beat 4 (Physical Collapse / Falling to Knees)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ✅ *Safe for short commands or declarations*.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:00–00:03] Full Shot, balanced full-body depth, Static eye-level framing as [Subject] stands rigid on rain-slick cobblestones, mud splattering heavy leather boots, broad shoulders locked in defiance...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Never cut off feet or ankles; cutting at the joints causes optical amputation artifacts in AI diffusion.

#### 5. Cowboy Shot (MLS / Trung Toàn Nghênh Chiến)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Frames character from **mid-thigh up** to the top of the head (Western standoff framing).
* **Optical Depth**: Staged depth showing character posture, hand placement, and immediate surroundings.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Moderate portrait-environmental lens (`crisp mid-range depth of field`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `6/10` to `8/10` (Latent violence, imminent duel).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 1 (Pre-Duel Standoff)**, **Beat 3 (Escalation to Weapons/Ultimatum)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ✅ *100% Safe*.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:00–00:03] Low-Angle Cowboy Shot, crisp mid-range depth, Slow forward push-in on [Subject] standing rigid from mid-thigh up, right leather-gloved hand hovering inches above the iron pommel at belt...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Never cut directly at the knee joint; framing MUST cut cleanly at mid-thigh.

#### 6. Medium Two-Shot (M2S / Trung Cảnh Hai Người Đối Đầu)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Both characters framed simultaneously from waist up, with the physical table, desk, or open space between them clearly delineated.
* **Optical Depth**: Wide focal plane holding both characters in sharp focus across the 1.5m–3m staging gap.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Standard natural perspective (`balanced depth maintaining dual subject sharpness`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `7/10` (Balanced power struggle).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 1 (Negotiation Opening)**, **Beat 3 (Tabletop Clash)**, **Beat 6 (Equal Reconciliation)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ✅ *100% Safe*. Perfect for continuous co-presence without cutting.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:00–00:03] Eye-Level Medium Two-Shot, balanced focal plane, Slow forward push-in: [Subject A] at frame-left faces [Subject B] at frame-right across the rough-hewn oak table, both with palms planted firmly on the grain...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Never let one character block the face of the other unless intentionally executing a foreground occlusion wipe.

#### 7. Over-The-Shoulder Shot (OTS / Góc Qua Bờ Vai Đối Thoại)
* **Framing Boundaries**: The outer shoulder, nape, or ear of Character A occupies the outer 1/3 foreground; Character B occupies the central 2/3 and is framed from chest or waist up.
* **Optical Depth**: Shallow depth of field on foreground shoulder; razor-sharp focus on the listening/speaking subject.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Portrait focal length (`cinematic shallow depth of field, soft foreground shoulder blur, crisp facial focus`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `7/10` to `9/10` (Interrogation pressure).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 3 (Adversarial Ping-Pong Dialogue Exchanges)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ✅ *100% Safe*. Standard workhorse for Track B Cut 1 and Cut 2.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:00–00:04] Over-The-Shoulder Shot, cinematic shallow depth, looking past [Subject A]'s dark blurred shoulder at [Subject B]'s tightening expression as dialogue lands...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Do not use in single-person monologue scenes where no second person is physically present.

#### 8. Medium Shot (MS / Trung Cảnh Thao Tác)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Frames character from the **waist up** to the crown of the head.
* **Optical Depth**: Natural human perspective with soft background falloff.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Standard prime lens equivalent (`natural human eye perspective, crisp tactile clarity`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `5/10` to `7/10` (Active drama and physical interaction).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 2 (Setup)**, **Beat 3 (Escalating Drama & Prop Usage)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ✅ *100% Safe*.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:03–00:07] Medium Shot, natural human eye perspective, Lateral tracking reframe as [Subject] slams both palms down on the table, leaning forward with rigid spine...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Avoid static, lifeless poses; Medium Shots demand motivated physical torso or arm action.

#### 9. Medium Close-Up (MCU / Trung Cận Đối Thoại)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Frames character from **mid-chest up** to the top of the head.
* **Optical Depth**: Moderate-to-shallow depth of field; background softly blurred while head, throat, and shoulders remain razor-sharp.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Portrait prime lens equivalent (`flattering portrait perspective, creamy background blur, sharp ocular and throat focus`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `6/10` to `8/10` (Workhorse of intense dialogue).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 1 (Disruptive Hook)**, **Beat 3 (Core Ideological Clash)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ✅ *100% Safe*. Recommended for 60%+ of all dialogue shots.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:00–00:03] Medium Close-Up, flattering portrait perspective, Eye-level static focus on [Subject] delivering ultimatum with rising chest tension and pulsing jaw muscles...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Do not cut off the chin or crown of the head; maintain proper cinematic headroom.

#### 10. Close-Up (CU / Cận Cảnh Gương Mặt)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Full human face from just below the chin to the crown of the head.
* **Optical Depth**: Shallow depth of field; background completely diffused into buttery bokeh.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Tight portrait prime lens equivalent (`cinematic shallow depth of field, creamy background blur, crisp focal lock on irises`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `8/10` to `9/10` (Peak emotional intimacy).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 4 (Climax Peak / Fatal Revelation)**, **Beat 5 (Catharsis)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ✅ *100% Safe for high-stakes lines*.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:03–00:07] Low-Angle Close-Up, creamy soft background blur, Slow forward push-in tightening on [Subject]'s clenched jaw, nostril flare, and unblinking amber glare catching warm brazier light...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Never introduce complex multi-hand physical actions in a Close-Up; the frame is reserved exclusively for facial biomechanics.

#### 11. Head-On Shot (Cảnh Trực Diện Xuyên Thấu Trục Ống Kính)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Symmetrical framing centered on the subject glaring or advancing directly down the optical lens axis.
* **Optical Depth**: Balanced-to-shallow depth with strong geometric one-point perspective lines.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Symmetrical prime lens equivalent (`geometric one-point central perspective`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `9/10` (Direct confrontation / Fourth-Wall breach).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 1 (Shock Hook)**, **Beat 4 (Defiant Ultimatum)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ✅ *100% Safe*.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:00–00:03] Head-On Shot, geometric central perspective, [Subject] marches straight down the central optical lens axis, piercing dark gaze locked dead-center on camera, zero lateral deviation...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Do not tilt the camera off-axis; symmetry and axial alignment are mandatory.

#### 12. Choker Shot (Cận Cảnh Ngột Ngạt)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Tight framing from **just below the lower lip/chin** up to the **upper forehead/eyebrows**. Cuts off both top of head and neck.
* **Optical Depth**: Razor-thin focal slice isolating eyes, nose bridge, and mouth.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: Ultra-tight macro-portrait lens (`razor-thin depth of field, zero background visibility`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `10/10` (Suffocating claustrophobia, cornered predator).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 3.3 (Exits Sealed)**, **Beat 4.3 (All Hope Is Lost)**, **Shot 3 (Arrest Living Settle)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ⚠️ *Restricted to short, choked phrases (1–4 words)* or complete diegetic silence.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:05.5–00:10] Choker Shot, razor-thin focal slice, Static locked focus on [Subject]'s quivering lower lip and flared nostrils as cold sweat beads on forehead, lips sealed in breathless deadlock from 00:09.5 to 00:10...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: NEVER assign multi-sentence speeches to a Choker Shot (causes severe AI mouth distortion and lip-sync failure).

#### 13. Extreme Close-Up (ECU / Đặc Tả Vi Mô)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Hyper-magnified macro framing on a single biological micro-detail: a single human eye and dilating pupil, trembling fingertip, scar ridge, tear spilling over eyelid.
* **Optical Depth**: Microscopic Depth of Field; foreground and background immediately blur into complete abstraction.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: True macro optical lens (`extreme physical magnification, hyper-magnified macro focus`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `10/10` (Visceral exposure of truth or biological shock).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 4 (Ego Death / Anagnorisis / Lie Exposure)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ❌ **FORBIDDEN FOR SPOKEN DIALOGUE**. Silent breath or macro Foley only.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:07–00:10] Extreme Close-Up, hyper-magnified macro focus, Static locked focus on [Subject]'s dark pupil dilating in paralyzed shock as heat embers reflect across the cornea...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: NEVER attempt spoken lip-sync in an ECU. NEVER use rapid camera pans.

#### 14. Macro Insert Shot (Insert / Đặc Tả Đạo Cụ & Bằng Chứng)
* **Framing Boundaries**: Hyper-magnified macro framing on a single inanimate prop: wax seal snapping, poison drop hitting wine surface, knife tip carving wood, bronze deed sliding.
* **Optical Depth**: Razor-thin Depth of Field focused strictly on the contact point between human skin and object material.
* **Lens Perspective Equivalent**: True macro lens (`razor-thin macro depth, tactile material focus`).
* **Psychological Pressure Level**: `7/10` to `9/10` (Chekhov's prop causality).
* **Story Beat Alignment**: **Beat 3 (Trap Detonation)**, **Beat 4 (Irrefutable Evidence)**.
* **Spoken Dialogue Safety**: ❌ **FORBIDDEN FOR ON-SCREEN DIALOGUE**. Voiceover or diegetic Foley only.
* **Prompt Syntax**:
  `[00:04–00:07] Macro Insert Shot, tactile macro focus, Static view as trembling calloused thumb forcefully cracks the crimson wax seal, brittle red flakes showering across aged parchment fibers...`
* 🚫 **Strict Prohibition**: Do not include human faces in a Macro Insert Shot; the object and hand contact are the sole focal points.

### 1.3. Scale Progression & Directorial Pairing Laws (Tiến Trình Cỡ Cảnh & Cặp Cắt Chuẩn Mực)

#### 1. The 30-Degree & Scale Jump Law (Luật Nhảy Cỡ Cảnh & Góc Máy)
When cutting between two shots in Track B (Multi-Shot Cut Sequence):
* **Rule 1**: NEVER cut between two identical shot scales of the same character (e.g. MCU to MCU) unless the camera angle shifts by at least **30 degrees**. Cutting between two identical scales with minimal angle change produces an amateur jump cut.
* **Rule 2**: The optimal emotional progression within a 10-second unit follows the **Tightening Spiral with Dynamic Timestamps**:
  - `Shot 1 [00:00–00:XX.Xs]`: **Medium Close-Up (MCU) or OTS** (Establishes stance, premise, and initial momentum).
  - `Shot 2 [00:XX.X–00:YY.Ys]`: **Counter MCU or Close-Up (CU)** (Inherits forward velocity, delivers verbal/physical blow, duration calibrated to dialogue length).
  - `Shot Transition & Pacing`: Flexible shot staging (1 continuous take, 2 dynamic shots, or rapid cuts) tailored to emotional tension; no mandatory 3-shot structure.

#### 2. Single Continuous Scale Modulation (Track A)
In Track A (Single-Shot Continuous Take), shot scales change dynamically through camera movement rather than cuts:
* **The Intimacy Push-In**: Camera moves forward from **Medium Shot (MS) $\to$ Medium Close-Up (MCU) $\to$ Close-Up (CU)** over 10 seconds.
* **The Existential Retreat**: Camera pulls backward from **Close-Up (CU) $\to$ Medium Shot (MS) $\to$ Extreme Wide Shot (EWS)** over 10 seconds.

## ⚖️ § 3. DIMENSION 4: CAMERA ACTIONS, ANGLES & MOVEMENTS (HÀNH VI & VẬN ĐỘNG MÁY QUAY)

### 3.0. The Angle Decision Doctrine — Whose Eyes, Whose Power

Shot scale decides **how close** the audience stands; the angle decides **from where** — and therefore whose side the audience is on and who holds power in the frame. The 4-axis tables below are a **vocabulary, not a lookup table**. Choose an angle by answering three questions about the specific moment, in this order:

1. **Whose moment is this?** (Alignment) Put the camera near the eyeline of the character whose experience the moment belongs to — or deliberately outside everyone (a detached observer) when the audience should judge rather than feel.
2. **Where is the power between these bodies right now?** Measure camera height against the **subject's eyes, not the floor**. In shot / reverse-shot, the height gap between the two setups *is* the power gap: looking up at A only reads as dominance when it is paired with looking down at B.
3. **Has alignment or power shifted since the previous frame?** A new angle must be earned. When power flips, the angles flip or converge — both setups at eye level reads as equality or reconciliation. If nothing shifted, keep the angle.

**Principles:**
- **Angles are relative.** "Eye level" means the subject's eye level. Kneeling, seated, lying, or infant-in-crib subjects pull the camera down with them. The absolute heights in the Pitch table (1.3 m, 0.9 m, 40 cm) assume a standing adult.
- **Vertical eyeline match.** If A stands and B kneels, A looks down and B looks up. A's single sits slightly below A's eyes and B's single slightly above B's, or the geography of the cut breaks.
- **Subtle carries more than extreme.** A few degrees off eye level shifts power while keeping the face readable. Reserve overhead, worm's-eye, nadir, and Dutch for moments where the character's world itself tilts — usually once per episode at most.
- **Meaning comes from context, not from the angle.** The same low angle reads as threat in one scene and as protection in a later one; that callback is an angle arc. Counter-use is legitimate: a low angle on a weeping character can grant dignity; a high angle on a victor can expose a hollow win. The "Usually Avoid" column below states a default, not a ban.
- **Movement changes the angle.** A crane rise or pedestal lift turns an eye-level shot into a high angle. Decide whether that diminishment is wanted at the instant it lands — do not drift above a character during the line that sets them free unless the intent is blessing or overview.
- **Anti-default check.** Generative models fall back to eye-level frontal medium when no angle is stated. If three consecutive scenes leave the angle unstated or sit at plain eye-level frontal, the episode has a template. Rethink at least one of them from question 1.

**Hard constraints (never yield):**
- **Lip-sync needs a visible mouth.** Spoken lines play frontal to three-quarter; profile only for short lines; three-quarter rear, full back, overhead, and nadir carry no lip-sync — use them for silence, V.O., or off-screen voice.
- **Axis discipline in Track B:** stay on one side of the 180° line, and respect the 30° rule for same-scale cuts on one character (§1.3).
- **No fast angle change under a spoken line:** whip pan, rapid tilt, or a travelling orbit must land before the line starts (§3.2, `0b-temporal-camera-grammar.md` §5).

**Prompt syntax:**
- Describe the angle through a **physical anchor**, not numbers: "camera at crib-mattress height, looking up at ma_ba's face", "from behind ma_ba's shoulder, looking down on minh_khang".
- For OTS and dirty singles, name what sits soft in the foreground so the model renders the relationship, not just the face.
- State the angle at the start of each phase, and again whenever a move changes it.

> **Worked angle arc (`com-khong-chan-nuoc-mat` Ep 1):** Scene 02 shoots Má Ba from floor level, looking up (the couple's fear), and Minh Khang from above (pinned). In Scene 05, when Má Ba breaks, her reverse drops to eye level — power has levelled. In Scene 10, the same low angle returns, now making her a shield for the crib. One angle, three meanings, earned by story.

### 3.2. Camera Movements & Trajectories (Động Học & Chuyển Động)

#### 3.2.0. The Movement Decision Doctrine — Motive, Landing, Timing

A camera move is felt as **the audience's attention travelling**. The table below is a vocabulary; durations and lip-sync rules are owned by `0b-temporal-camera-grammar.md` §5. Decide every move with three questions, in this order:

1. **Why move at all?** Name one motive from Creative Doctrine §2.3: **(a)** follow attention (a glance, a rise, a walk), **(b)** reveal information, **(c)** express an inner shift. No motive → a locked frame. Stillness is a choice, not an absence.
2. **Where does it land?** Decide the **landing frame first** — scale, angle, and who is in it — then the path. The landing must differ from the start in information or relationship; a move that ends where it began is decoration.
3. **When does it start and stop?** Start on a trigger (a character moves, looks, or a sound cuts in). Stop **before** the line or beat that needs stillness. Respect the minimum stable durations in 0b §5; if the move cannot fit, start it earlier on a trigger in the previous phase instead of rushing it.

**Principles:**
- **Subject-led vs camera-led.** A move that follows a body or a glance is invisible; a move the camera makes on its own (push-in on a still face, drift away from a lonely figure) is the director speaking. Spend camera-led moves on inner shifts only.
- **Direction carries meaning, context decides which.** Toward = pressure or intimacy; away = isolation or judgment; rising = overview, blessing, or diminishment; descending = gravity, fall, humility; lateral = accompanying or observing; arc = re-reading the power geometry.
- **Stillness gives motion its force.** If every scene glides, motion becomes wallpaper. Plan where the episode's static frames are — the peak is often one of them.
- **Budget.** Track A: at most 2 chained moves per 10 s; Track B: at most 1 move per shot (0b §5). A compound move (arc while rising) counts as one only when it has one motive and one landing.

**AI prompt craft:**
- One camera verb per sentence, with a speed word (*slow, unhurried, swift*) and a named landing ("…and settles on a frontal medium close-up of my_duyen").
- When the subject makes a big body move, keep the camera simple — follow it or stay locked. Two complex trajectories at once invite morphing.
- Parallax needs a named foreground layer (crib slats, a doorframe, a shoulder).
- **An unstated move is a move the model chooses for you** (usually a drifting glide). Write "locked frame" when stillness is intended.
- Write the timing into the prose: "the arc settles before minh_khang speaks".

**Hard constraints:** lip-sync compatibility per move (0b §5), no freeze frames, minimum stable durations.

| Movement | Mechanical Vector | Velocity & Dynamic | Directorial Purpose | Best Story Scenario | Lip-Sync Safety |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Static** | Zero movement on all axes. | 0.0 m/s | Solemn gravity, unblinking witness, aftermath truth. | Post-climax silence, corpses, signed treaties. | ✅ 100% Safe |

### 3.3. The 360-Degree Orbit Directorial Protocol (Quy Chuẩn Cú Xoay 360°)

```
                                    [Top-Down View of 360° Orbit]
                                            00:05 (Back 180°)
                                                  ▲
                                                  │
                 00:03 (Right 90°)  ◄──── [SUBJECT — dead center]  ────►  00:07 (Left 270°)
                                                  │
                                                  ▼
                                            00:00 / 00:10
                                          (Front Face 0°/360°)
                                    [Dialogue Delivery & Living Settle]
```

1. **Rotational Velocity Formula**:
   $$\mathbf{Angular\ Velocity} = 
rac{360^\circ}{10\text{ seconds}} = \mathbf{36^\circ / \text{second}}$$
   - **Beat 1 `[00:00–00:03]`**: Arc $0^\circ \to 90^\circ$ (Front face $\to$ Right shoulder).
   - **Beat 2 `[00:03–00:07]`**: Arc $90^\circ \to 270^\circ$ (Right shoulder $\to$ Full back $\to$ Left shoulder, revealing the background encirclement/threat).
   - **Beat 3 `[00:07–00:10]`**: Arc $270^\circ \to 360^\circ$ (Left shoulder $\to$ Front face complete lock).
2. **Subject Central Axis Lock**: The subject MUST serve as static rotational center anchored at the dead center of the frame.
3. **Lip-Sync & Dialogue Synchronization Rule in 360° Orbit**:
   - **CRITICAL**: Spoken dialogue lines MUST NEVER be placed during the fast-rotating back phase ($00:00–00:07$).
   - Spoken dialogue is strictly synchronized to the **final static lock phase `[00:07–00:10]`** when the camera completes the circle back to the front face and settles into the **organic continuous motion (zero frozen frames)**.

### 3.4. THE 7 HOLLYWOOD MASTER CINEMATIC COMBOS (ĐA DẠNG HÓA THỊ GIÁC & DIỆT TRỪ RẬP KHUÔN)

To eliminate visual monotony and generic formulaic shot repetition (e.g. repetitively doing OTS $\to$ Close-Up $\to$ Choker), the director selects one of the **7 Hollywood Master Combos** tailored to scene emotion:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                 THE 7 HOLLYWOOD MASTER COMBOS                                          │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. THE INTIMIDATION TILT    : Low-Angle Cowboy ➔ Vertical Tilt-Up ➔ Low-Angle Close-Up                │
│ 2. THE ISOLATION PULL-OUT   : High-Angle Medium ➔ Dolly Pull-Out ➔ Extreme Wide Shot                  │
│ 3. THE CHESSBOARD OVERHEAD  : 90° Overhead (God's Eye) ➔ Snap-Cut ➔ 25° Dutch Angle                   │
│ 4. THE VERTIGO REVELATION   : Close-Up ➔ Vertigo Effect (Dolly-Zoom) ➔ Extreme Close-Up (ECU)         │
│ 5. THE 360° SURROUNDING ORBIT: Steadicam 360° Orbit Medium Shot (36°/s)                               │
│ 6. THE SUBJECTIVE SCANNING  : Foreground Hands Insert ➔ Vertical Tilt-Up ➔ Low-Angle Opponent Glance   │
│ 7. THE WHISTLEBLOWER VLOG   : Arm's-Length MCU Selfie ➔ Gentle Handheld ➔ Direct Address              │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Combo 1: The Intimidation Tilt (Quyền Lực Đè Bẹp)
* **Shot Scale & Angle**: Low-Angle Cowboy Shot $\to$ Vertical Tilt-Up $\to$ Low-Angle Close-Up.
* **Directorial Intent**: Establishes overwhelming physical and psychological dominance. The camera sweeps from leather boots and hem upward to the cold, arrogant gaze, making the antagonist tower like an impenetrable fortress.
* **Best Used For**: Debt enforcers entering room, kings issuing unbendable decrees, villains demanding immediate surrender.

#### Combo 2: The Isolation Pull-Out (Cô Đơn & Bỏ Rơi)
* **Shot Scale & Angle**: High-Angle Medium Shot $\to$ Slow Dolly Pull-Out $\to$ Extreme Wide Shot.
* **Directorial Intent**: Captures utter abandonment and existential grief. As the camera retreats into the vast rafters, the fallen human figure shrinks into a tiny speck consumed by cavernous architectural darkness.
* **Best Used For**: Protagonist after total defeat, mourning beside a corpse, realization of irreversible loss.

#### Combo 3: The Chessboard Overhead & Dutch Angle (Bàn Cờ Đấu Trí)
* **Shot Scale & Angle**: 90° Overhead Shot (God's Eye) $\to$ Snap-Cut to 25° Dutch Angle.
* **Directorial Intent**: Establishes cold, clinical tactical calculation as documents and deeds are examined from the ceiling, followed by a violent cognitive shock when the camera tilts 25° as the legal trap is sprung.
* **Best Used For**: Contract betrayals, courtroom ambush evidence, tactical planning sessions.

#### Combo 4: The Vertigo Revelation (Sốc Địa Chấn)
* **Shot Scale & Angle**: Close-Up $\to$ Vertigo Effect (Dolly-Zoom) $\to$ Extreme Close-Up (ECU).
* **Directorial Intent**: The subject's face maintains scale while the background is sucked into a warped, yawning vortex. Conveys the sensation of the earth collapsing beneath one's feet upon learning a mortal truth.
* **Best Used For**: Discovering that the mentor is the murderer, realizing one has poisoned their own child, unexpected treason.

#### Combo 5: The 360° Surrounding Orbit (Vòng Vây Bủa Vây)
* **Shot Scale & Angle**: Steadicam 360° Orbital Medium Shot ($36^\circ/\text{s}$).
* **Directorial Intent**: The camera sweeps a continuous circle around the isolated protagonist at center-frame, catching the sneering silhouettes of accusers encircling them on all sides before locking back on the protagonist's defiant eyes.
* **Best Used For**: Facing a hostile council, public trial in a mobbed town square, hero surrounded by drawn blades.

#### Combo 6: The Subjective Scanning P.O.V. (P.O.V Đo Đạc & Đối Đầu)
* **Shot Scale & Angle**: Foreground Hands Insert $\to$ Vertical Tilt-Up $\to$ Low-Angle Opponent Eye Contact.
* **Directorial Intent**: Operates directly through the protagonist's eyes. Starts with the character inspecting their own bruised knuckles or held key in the lower frame, then snaps upward to confront the towering enemy staring directly down the lens.
* **Best Used For**: Counter-attack preparations, cornered interrogations, moments before striking back.

#### Combo 7: The Whistleblower Handheld Vlog (Vlog Tự Thú Điều Tra)
* **Shot Scale & Angle**: Arm's-length wide Medium Close-Up Selfie $\to$ Gentle handheld drift $\to$ Direct Address.
* **Directorial Intent**: Authenticity of an urgent live broadcast. The character holds the device, walking under night rain or neon backlight, whispering undeniable evidence directly into the lens before the feed is violently terminated.
* **Best Used For**: Investigative whistleblowers, final confession tapes, modern conspiracy exposures.

### 4.1. Deep Dive: In-Camera Transitions (Zero-Cut 10s Takes)
1. **The Kinetic Prop Bridge (Tracking Pan via Prop)**:
   - *Mechanics*: Character A hurls, slides, or drops a physical object; camera tracks the prop's momentum, panning across the floor/table until it strikes Character B's boots or hands.
   - *Prompt Syntax*: `Kaelen hurls the bronze signet across the flagstones; camera executes a fluid tracking pan following the spinning bronze disc as it sparks against stone and clatters into Tarik's iron boots.`
2. **Steadicam Arc Pivot (90°–180° Orbital Glide)**:
   - *Mechanics*: Camera begins behind Character A's shoulder, glides smoothly in a semi-circular arc around the table, descending to frame Character B's face directly.
   - *Prompt Syntax*: `Steadicam executes a descending 120-degree orbital arc pivot around Kaelen's shoulder, sweeping across the tabletop to reframe tightly on Tarik rising from shadow.`
3. **Optical Rack Focus (Focus Pull)**:
   - *Mechanics*: Static camera shifts optical focal plane from Subject A's trembling knuckles in the foreground to Subject B's cold smirk in the blurred background.
   - *Prompt Syntax*: `Static locked camera; optical rack focus shifts sharpness from the leaking poison vial on the foreground table to the Duchess's unblinking cold gaze in the blurred background.`
4. **Vertical Elevation Tilt (Dais to Floor)**:
   - *Mechanics*: Camera tilts smoothly down a vertical axis (e.g. from queen on high throne down to kneeling general).
   - *Prompt Syntax*: `Low-angle vertical tilt descends smoothly from the Queen seated on the high golden throne down the marble steps to land tightly on the kneeling General bound in chains.`

## 🏛️ PART IV: 3D SPATIAL GEOMETRY & VECTOR-TO-PLANE BLOCKING

## 🏛️ 1. THE ROOT PROBLEM: 2D LATENT HALLUCINATION VS 3D EUCLIDEAN PHYSICS

Generative AI video diffusion models do **NOT** possess an inherent 3D physics engine or an internal spatial CAD coordinate system. They operate purely on 2D latent pixel probability distributions learned from video clips.

When a human or AI agent writes a generic prompt like:  
❌ *"Faelen falls from above and gets caught in the spiderweb..."*  
The video model hallucinates the most common training representation of a spiderweb: **a vertical curtain strung between two vertical tree trunks**.

### 💥 The Fatal Spatial Contradiction:
```
               [Faelen falling along -Y Vertical Vector]

## 🏛️ PART V: MULTI-SCENE CONTINUITY & THE CONTINUITY LEDGER

## Core Concept: The Clip Is Not a Scene

In traditional screenwriting, a "scene" is defined by continuous space + continuous time and can run for minutes. In AI-generated multi-clip video, the atomic unit is different:

**A clip = one action beat = one state change.**

A clip should begin at a defined state (State A) and end at a different, recognizably distinct state (State B) — a change in position, pose, expression, camera framing, or lighting. Never plan a clip around a time duration ("give me 10 seconds of walking"); plan it around what *changes* during that duration.

**Rule of thumb:** if you cannot name what is different between the first and last frame of a planned clip, it is not a well-formed clip — either merge it with a neighbor or split it further.

**Anti-pattern to flag:** cramming 2-3 distinct actions into a single clip (e.g., "opens the door, walks in, and looks around") almost always produces a rushed, visually confused clip, because the generation model has to compress multiple state changes into a few seconds. Split into separate clips instead.

**Worked example:**

❌ Bad — one clip, four state changes stacked:
> "Character opens the door, walks into the room, looks around, then sits down in the chair."
> (door closed→open) + (outside→inside) + (looking forward→looking around) + (standing→sitting) — four separate changes forced into one clip. The model has to compress all four into a few seconds, usually producing rushed or dropped actions.

✅ Good — split into four clips, one state change each:

| Clip | Start State | End State | State Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Standing at closed door | Door open, hand still on handle | Closed → Open |
| 2 | Standing at threshold | Fully inside, standing mid-room | Outside → Inside |
| 3 | Standing, looking straight ahead | Head turned, scanning the room | Forward gaze → Scanning |
| 4 | Standing beside chair | Seated | Standing → Sitting |

Each clip's End State becomes the next clip's Start State — the handoff is unambiguous because each clip only changed one thing.

## Continuity Theory (from film script supervision)

These are long-established live-action filmmaking principles that transfer directly and are the single highest-leverage knowledge to give the agent.

- **Continuity of action / match on action:** the pose, motion, and momentum of a subject at the end of clip N should logically match its state at the start of clip N+1. A character mid-stride at the end of one clip should be mid-stride (same leg forward) at the start of the next, not reset to a static stance.
- **180-degree rule:** once an imaginary line is established through the main axis of action (e.g., the direction two characters are facing each other, or the direction of travel), the camera should stay on one side of that line across the clip sequence. Crossing it without a deliberate transition disorients the viewer and reads as a spatial "jump" even if each clip is individually well-made.
- **Eyeline match:** if a subject is looking in a direction at the end of a clip, the next clip should pay off or continue that direction of attention (what they're looking at, or continuing to move that way).
- **Continuity of wardrobe/props/setting details:** clothing, held objects, hairstyles, lighting direction, and background elements must not change between clips unless the script explicitly calls for it (a costume change, a time skip, weather changing). Any change must be *motivated* by the story, never accidental.
- **Screen direction consistency:** if a subject or vehicle exits frame-right in one clip, it should generally enter frame-left in the next (standard convention for "continuing the same direction of travel"), unless the script uses a reversal deliberately.

**Agent checklist per handoff between clips** — before finalizing a clip transition, verify:
1. Position/pose continuity
2. Direction of movement / screen direction
3. Eyeline / gaze direction
4. Wardrobe, props, hairstyle
5. Lighting direction and color temperature
6. Time of day / weather
7. Camera side of the 180-degree line

## Multi-Subject Scenes (2-3+ Characters)

The "one state change per clip" rule does **not** become "one state change per character" when multiple characters share a clip. It generalizes to:

**1 clip = 1 event/interaction, regardless of how many characters participate in that event.**

Two characters shaking hands is *one* state change (not-touching → touching) even though two people are involved, because it is one causally-linked event.

**Anti-pattern:** two independent, unrelated actions happening in parallel within the same clip — e.g., "Character A walks in and sits down, while Character B stands up and walks to the door." These are two unrelated state changes that just happen to overlap in time, not one event. Split into separate clips, even if it means the two characters are temporarily not shown "simultaneously" — the sequence can still imply simultaneity through pacing without forcing both into one clip.

**Valid multi-character clip:** an action-and-reaction pair, where one character's change is *caused by* or *directly responds to* the other's — e.g., "Character B stands up and turns toward Character A, who has just entered." This is still one beat, because B's motion is a direct causal reaction to A's presence, not an unrelated parallel action.

**Rules for scripting 3+ characters:**
1. **Identify a primary subject (driver) per clip.** Even with three people in frame, one person's action should be the beat's driver; the others are in a relatively static or reactive state.
2. **Track each character's continuity independently.** Wardrobe, position, and gaze direction must be verified per-character at every checkpoint — do not let one character's continuity data bleed into another's.
3. **Block by interaction thread.** If three characters have two separate interaction threads (A talks to B, C observes from a distance), script them as separate clips per thread rather than forcing all three into one compound event.
4. **Eyelines get harder to track with 3+ subjects.** With two characters, only one eyeline pair needs to match. With three or more, explicitly state who is looking at whom at the end of every clip — this is one of the most common generation failure points as character count grows.

## Beat Sheet Structure for Short-Form Chained Video

Because each clip is short, the overall script should be organized as a **beat sheet**, not full-scene prose description.

- **Beat:** the smallest unit of story movement — a mini-arc of setup → micro-tension or intention → micro-resolution/transition, playable in a single clip.
- **Mini-arc grouping:** group beats into clusters of roughly 3-5 clips that form a complete mini-arc (setup → rising action → small peak/turn → transition out). This mirrors how three-act structure or Save-the-Cat-style beat sheets scale down: instead of one large arc across the whole runtime, the sequence becomes a chain of small arcs, each internally satisfying, that also connect to the next.
- Reference frameworks the agent can draw on for pacing logic: three-act structure, Save the Cat beat sheet, and general short-form/commercial editing pacing (where cognitive load per shot is much higher per second than in long-form film, so each beat must be legible fast).

**Information density rule:** a viewer can absorb roughly one to two new pieces of visual information per short clip (new setting, new character, complex action). A clip introducing a new location, a new character, AND a complex action simultaneously will almost always fail to read clearly — both to a human viewer and to the generation model attempting to render it. Apply a "one dominant idea per clip" discipline, the same principle used in slide design ("one idea per slide").

## 🌐 Universal Spatial Dynamics: Interior, Exterior & Cross-Space Threshold Mechanics

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **SPATIAL CONTINUITY & OPTICAL COHERENCE IN GENERATIVE VIDEO**:
> Video diffusion models (Sora, Kling, Runway, Hailuo) handle physical boundaries differently from traditional cameras. Directing across spaces demands strict optical rules:

### 1. Pure Interior Staging (INT.)
- **Geometric Bounding Planes**: Wall boundaries, ceilings, and floor surfaces (epoxy, terrazzo, wooden floorboards) must remain fixed.
- **Lighting Dynamics**: Directional key light from interior fixtures (LED strip lights, floor lamps, ceiling lights) balanced by soft window bounces. Sharp floor reflections anchor spatial realism.
- **Enclosed Acoustic Signature**: Distinct room reverberation, footsteps, and localized diegetic Foley.

### 2. Pure Exterior Staging (EXT.)
- **Infinite Focal Depth & Natural Vectors**: Celestial lighting (overhead sunlight, golden sunset angle, overcast diffuse sky) governs all shadows.
- **Aerodynamic Forces**: Environmental winds must tangibly affect loose materials (billowing coat hems, fluttering hair strands, swaying tree branches).
- **Acoustic Openness**: Unbounded acoustic space (distant urban rumble, rustling foliage, howling wind).

### 3. Cross-Space & Threshold Transitions (Giao Thoa 2 Không Gian Khác Nhau)
When a scene bridges an indoor and outdoor environment (e.g. from a room out onto a veranda/balcony, or looking through glass):
- **Track A: Single-Shot Threshold Push-Through (Cú Máy Xuyên Ngưỡng Cửa)**:
  * Camera moves with subject from an interior space directly through open sliding glass doors or an open portal into the exterior.
  * **Exposure Adaptation Law**: The prompt MUST describe camera optical exposure adjustment (`camera tracks through the glass doorway into brilliant exterior sunlight, exposure adapting smoothly from indoor LED ambiance to natural daylight`) to prevent AI latent clipping and white-out blown highlights.
  * **Connected Spatial Context**: Context is declared as an integrated threshold (`khong_gian_lien_hoan [100% Refer to...] spanning from interior room through open glass sliding doors onto the outdoor sunlit terrace`).
- **Track B: Multi-Shot Cross-Space Intercut (Cắt Cảnh Đối Lập Qua Ranh Giới)**:
  * Shot 1 frames Subject A indoors looking outward through a physical transparent barrier (window, sliding glass, threshold).
  * Shot 2 cuts across the 180° Action Axis framing Subject B outdoors looking back inward.
  * **180° Eyeline Match**: Screen directions must remain locked (Subject A screen-left looking screen-right; Subject B screen-right looking screen-left).
  * **Lighting Contrast Delta**: Indoor shot features tungsten/LED key with exterior bright background; outdoor shot features natural celestial key with dark interior reflections in the glass.

### 4. Confined & Intimate Spaces (Không Gian Hẹp: Ngôi Nhà Nhỏ, Hang Động, Cabin, Ngưỡng Cửa)
> [!IMPORTANT]
> **THE CONFINED SPACE PARADOX IN GENERATIVE AI**:
> When staging in physically tight environments (small rustic house, narrow cave mouth, compact kitchen, small wooden cabin), wide shots trigger catastrophic diffusion failures: the AI hallucinates unnatural cavernous dimensions, distorts walls with fisheye warping, or merges characters into background clutter.
> Directing in confined spaces requires **Intelligent Focal Depth, Progressive Shot Scaling, and Spatial Framing**:

#### A. The 3 Golden Rules of Confined Space Staging:
1. **Ban Unmotivated Wide Shots**: Never use an Extreme Wide Shot inside a small 15m² room or narrow cave. Use **Medium Close-Up (MCU)**, **Tight Medium (MS)**, and **Tactile Inserts (CU/ECU)** to convey intimacy, claustrophobia, or rustic coziness without exposing AI boundary limitations.
2. **Frame-Within-a-Frame (Khung Hình Trong Khung Hình)**: Utilize natural architectural apertures (doorframes, low cave ceilings, wooden lintels, window mullions) as foreground silhouettes. This anchors depth and prevents wall warping.
3. **The Reverse Threshold Looking-Out Angle (Góc Nhìn Ngược Từ Trong Nhìn Ra)**: Position the camera INSIDE the darker compact space looking out through the door/mouth at the brightly lit exterior. The subject enters as a dramatic silhouette catching sharp separation rim light, creating maximum dynamic contrast without needing massive room volume.

#### B. The Threshold Access Action Chain (Case Study: Unlocking & Entering a Small House):
To depict a character approaching, unlocking, and entering a small house or cavern without AI hand-morphing or spatial jumps:
- **Track A: Single-Shot Unbroken Steadicam Follow (00:00–00:10)**:
  * `[00:00–00:04.0]`: Medium three-quarter tracking shot following the character up the porch steps, closing distance to the wooden door. The camera tilts subtly down to frame the hand raising brass keys toward the keyhole.
  * `[00:04.0–00:07.0]`: Smooth push-in to an intimate close-up as the key inserts into the brass lock with a crisp mechanical snap, the hand turning the latch and pushing the heavy timber door inward. Outdoor light slices across the narrow interior entrance.
  * `[00:07.0–00:10.0]`: Camera glides forward across the raised wooden threshold behind the character's shoulder, entering the compact interior. Exposure adapts to warm ambient indoor shadow; the character pulls the door half-closed behind her, settling into a deep, relieved biological breath.
- **Track B: Multi-Shot Kinetic Threshold Montage (3 Beats)**:
  * `Beat 1 [00:00–00:03.5] (EXT)`: Eye-level Medium Tracking shot on the exterior porch: The woman advances briskly to the weathered door frame under natural daylight.
  * `Beat 2 [00:03.5–00:06.5] (THRESHOLD INSERT)`: Low-angle Extreme Close-Up on the door lock: Slender fingers slide a brass key into the keyway, twisting 90 degrees with an audible metallic click, pushing the lock bolt back as the door unlatches.
  * `Beat 3 [00:06.5–00:10.0] (INT)`: Low-angle Interior Reverse Shot looking out: Positioned inside the dim small room looking out the open doorway. The woman crosses the threshold as a rim-lit silhouette against the sunlit porch, stepping onto the floorboards and pulling the wooden door shut behind her, ambient room dust settling in the light shaft right to 00:10.0, zero frozen frames.

## The Continuity Ledger (State Tracking) & Anti-Ghost Prop Law

### 👻 The Anti-Ghost Prop Law & Single Instance Lock (Luật Chống Đạo Cụ Ma & Nhân Bản)
> [!CAUTION]
> **DIFFUSION MODELS DO NOT PERSIST A 3D WORLD STATE — AVOID DUAL PROMPT CLONING**:
> In generative diffusion video models (Kling, Runway, Sora, Wan 2.1), the text encoder (CLIP / T5-XXL) processes all prompt tokens in a single semantic pass.
> If a prop is introduced at Location A (e.g. `lying on the tiled floor`) and subsequently handled at Location B in Shot 2 without explicitly clearing Location A, the diffusion model interprets both statements simultaneously and renders **TWO INSTANCES OF THE SAME PROP** (one in the hand, one still lying on the floor).
>
> **THE 4 PILLARS OF GHOST-PROP ERADICATION**:
> 1. **Single Instance Lock**: Exactly ONE physical instance of any interactive prop exists in the story universe.
> 2. **Explicit Resting Surface Clearance**: Whenever a character picks up or shifts a prop, the prompt MUST explicitly declare that the resting surface is clear (e.g., `lifts the wrench, leaving the floor tiles completely clear and empty`).
> 3. **Stationary Spatial Anchor Law for Heavy Fixtures**: Heavy furniture, large fixtures, and major architectural elements (Pine Crib, Bed, Dining Table, Ancestral Altar) are permanently locked to their designated room/space coordinates across all scenes. They CANNOT teleport between rooms across scenes (e.g., if a pine crib is anchored beside the master bed, subsequent family interactions must occur in the bedroom or respect its stationary placement).
> 4. **Scene-to-Scene Chain of Custody**: The resting state of every prop at `00:10.0` of Scene N is the exact physical opening state at `00:00.0` of Scene N+1.

This is the digitized version of a film production's **continuity bible** (traditionally kept by the script supervisor). It is the single most important structural artifact for multi-clip generation, because clips are usually generated somewhat independently and nothing "remembers" state automatically the way a live film shoot does.

### Do not nest everything under Character

A common mistake is treating location, lighting, time of day, and props as sub-fields *inside* each character's entry. This breaks down as soon as a scene has more than one character or any object that outlives a single character's involvement:

- **Location/lighting/weather belong to the scene, not to a person.** If they're duplicated per-character, two characters in the same room can drift out of sync with each other (one entry says "living room," the other accidentally says "kitchen").
- **Props can change hands.** A cup handed from Character A to Character B is a transfer — if the prop only exists as a sub-field of one character, there's no clean way to represent the handoff.
- **Time of day and weather apply to everyone and everything in frame at once** — they should be updated in exactly one place, not copied into every character's block.

### Correct structure: three independent, parallel entity types

**1. Character Entities** — only what is physically attached to that person.
```
Entity: Character A
  - Fixed Description: (permanent appearance — never drifts)
  - Current Pose/Position/Expression: (updates every clip)
  - Wearing: (clothing/accessories currently on the body)
  - Holding: (reference to a Prop Entity — do not re-describe the prop here)
  - Last Updated At: clip #
```

**2. Prop/Object Entities** — tracked independently so they can be picked up, put down, or transferred without touching character data.
```
Entity: Prop 1
  - Fixed Description: (permanent appearance — never drifts)
  - Current Holder: (a Character Entity, "no one"/set down, or a location)
  - Current Condition: (full/empty, open/closed, intact/damaged, etc.)
  - Last Updated At: clip #
```

**3. Scene Environment Entities** — one shared entry per distinct setting, referenced by every character/prop present in it. Do not duplicate per character.
```
Entity: Environment 1
  - Location:
  - Time of Day:
  - Lighting Direction & Color Temperature:
  - Weather:
  - Fixed Background Elements:
  - Last Updated At: clip #
```

When a clip involves multiple characters, the agent reads **one shared Environment entity** plus each relevant **Character Entity** separately — never a copy of the environment nested inside each character.

### Categories of change

Two categories of change must be explicitly distinguished, and this distinction applies across all three entity types, not just characters:

- **Plot arc change** — a change the story *wants*: a character getting soaked in a rainstorm, a door going from closed to open, day turning to night, a prop changing hands. This is intentional and should be written into the ledger deliberately, with the clip that causes it identified.
- **Visual identity drift** — a change that should NOT happen: hair color shifting, a logo changing, a face looking subtly different, clothing changing color without narrative reason, an environment's lighting shifting with no scripted cause. This is a generation error to catch, not a story beat.

When scripting each new clip, the agent should read the ledger's current state for every entity appearing in that clip (character, prop, and environment), use it as the mandatory starting condition, then update the ledger with the new ending condition once the clip is defined.

**Checkpointing:** for long sequences, insert a checkpoint every 3-5 clips where the ledger state for every entity type is explicitly re-verified against its *original* fixed description (not just the previous clip), because small per-clip errors compound across a chain (drift accumulates). If a real generation shows an entity has drifted too far from its fixed description, the correct recovery is to regenerate from the last clean checkpoint, not to keep chaining forward from the drifted state.

## Transition Grammar

Different scripted transitions between clips carry different risk profiles for AI generation, and the agent should choose deliberately rather than defaulting to a straight cut every time:

- **Match cut:** the ending state of clip N is nearly identical in framing/pose to the starting state of clip N+1. Lowest risk, most seamless, should be the default choice whenever the action allows it.
- **Cutaway:** insert a short secondary clip (a close-up of an object, a detail, a hand) between two riskier clips. Useful as a *deliberate* tool to bridge a difficult continuity jump (e.g., a location change, a costume change, a big lighting shift) — the cutaway gives the sequence a place to "reset" without the viewer noticing a discontinuity in the main subject.
- **Jump cut:** an intentional, noticeable skip (time compression, "later that day"). This should only ever be a *scripted narrative choice*, never used to paper over a failed continuity match — if a transition is jarring because generation drifted, that is a production error, not a valid jump cut.

**Rule for the agent:** at every clip boundary, explicitly decide which of these three transition types is intended, and if match cut is not achievable given the story content, insert a cutaway on purpose rather than leaving an unplanned hard discontinuity.

## Ending a Clip Well (End-State Design)

Because the last frame/state of a clip becomes the anchor for the next (whether via literal frame-chaining or via description in a text prompt), clips should be scripted to **end at a legible, relatively stable moment**:

- Avoid scripting a clip to end mid-fast-motion or mid-motion-blur (e.g., mid-turn, mid-run, mid-gesture) — these produce ambiguous or corrupted end states that are hard to continue from cleanly.
- Prefer ending on a brief settle: a completed gesture, a held pose, a stopped movement, a clear gaze — even half a second of near-stillness at the end of a beat makes the next clip's starting condition unambiguous.
- If the story genuinely requires ending on fast motion (e.g., a chase), script an explicit cutaway or a deliberate jump cut immediately after, rather than trying to chain directly off a blurred end state.

## Script Deliverable Format

A well-formed script has **two layers**: a one-time Entity Registry, and a Clip Table that records only what *changes* (deltas), never full re-descriptions. This mirrors the ledger structure above and is the format the agent should default to when actually writing a script.

### Layer 1 — Entity Registry (declared once, at the top of the script)

Declare every recurring Character, Prop, and Environment exactly once, using the three-entity-type structure from the Continuity Ledger section. Nothing in this registry should ever be re-typed inside the Clip Table — the table only references entity names and records deltas against this baseline.

```
CHARACTERS
CHARACTER A — [name]
Fixed Description: ...

CHARACTER B — [name]
Fixed Description: ...

PROPS
PROP 1 — [name]
Fixed Description: ...

ENVIRONMENTS
ENV 1 — [name]
Lighting/Time of Day/Weather: ...
Space: ...

ENV 2 — [name]
Lighting/Time of Day/Weather: ...
Space: ...
```

Also declare **global constants** once — visual style, aspect ratio/format intentions, overall lighting mood — which hold across the entire sequence unless a specific clip calls for a deliberate shift.

### Layer 2 — Clip Table (delta-only)

| Clip | Entities Present | Main Event (1 state change) | Env | Prop Change | End State (handoff) | Transition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

- **Entities Present** — reference entity names/labels from the Registry only. Never re-describe an entity's fixed appearance here.
- **Main Event** — one sentence, one dominant event, imperative voice. Per the Multi-Subject Scenes section, this may involve multiple characters as long as it is one causally-linked event.
- **Env** — the Environment entity active for this clip. Leave as-is (or mark with a dash) if unchanged from the previous clip; only write a transition (`ENV 1 → ENV 2`) when the environment actually changes, and treat any such change as a high-risk boundary requiring a deliberate Transition choice (see below).
- **Prop Change** — leave blank if no prop changed hands or condition this clip. Only fill in when a Prop Entity's `Current Holder` or `Current Condition` changes (e.g., `Prop 1: B → A`).
- **End State (handoff)** — the specific, non-blurred ending condition this clip hands to the next; this becomes the next clip's implicit Start State and should never need to be re-typed there.
- **Transition** — match cut / cutaway / jump cut, chosen deliberately per the Transition Grammar section. Mandatory to specify explicitly whenever Env changes.

**Why delta-only, not full-redescription:** if every clip row re-describes each character's full appearance, wardrobe, and the room's lighting, the script both bloats and becomes a second, competing source of truth against the Entity Registry — any edit has to be made in two places and they will eventually disagree. Delta-only tables keep the Registry as the single source of truth and make every row auditable at a glance: scanning the Prop Change and Env columns alone tells the agent exactly where the continuity risk points are in the whole sequence.

**Common structural error to flag:** if a drafted Clip Table row contains a full character description ("a man, 30s, black hair...") instead of just a name/label, that's a sign the delta discipline has broken down — correct it back to a reference plus only what changed.

## Common Failure Patterns to Flag During Script Review

The agent should proactively flag these when reviewing or writing a script, since they are the most common root causes of a broken final sequence:

1. **Compound beats** — more than one state change packed into a single clip.
2. **Silent drift** — a fixed-description entity changing between clips with no narrative reason and no ledger flag.
3. **Blurred handoffs** — a clip scripted to end mid-fast-motion, making the next clip's starting condition ambiguous.
4. **Missing transition intent** — no explicit decision about match cut / cutaway / jump cut at a risky boundary, leaving continuity to chance.
5. **Overloaded first clip of a new setting** — introducing a new location, new character, and new action all in the clip immediately after a location change (should be split, or eased in with a cutaway/establishing beat).
6. **No checkpoint entities** — a long chain (6+ clips) with no point where state is re-verified against the original fixed description, allowing drift to compound silently.
7. **180-degree violations across clip boundaries** — camera side flipping between clips of the same continuous action without a motivated reason.
8. **Environment nested inside character** — location/lighting/weather duplicated per-character instead of tracked once as a shared Environment entity, risking two characters in the same scene silently disagreeing on where or when they are.
9. **Untracked prop handoffs** — a prop changing hands between characters with no explicit `Current Holder` update, leaving it ambiguous who is holding what in the next clip.
10. **Parallel unrelated actions forced into one clip** — two characters doing unconnected things at the same time crammed into a single clip instead of split into separate, sequential beats.
11. **Full re-description inside the Clip Table** — a clip row re-typing a character's or environment's full fixed description instead of referencing the Entity Registry and recording only the delta; creates a second, driftable source of truth.
12. **Missing eyeline resolution with 3+ characters** — a multi-character clip that doesn't specify who is looking at whom by the end state, left to chance as character count grows.

## Macro Continuity: Inter-Episode Causality & Living Realism Tracking

In multi-episode series production, continuity extends beyond clip-to-clip transitions within a single scene. The Agent must enforce **Macro Continuity across episode boundaries**:

### 1. The Inter-Episode Kinetic Hand-Off Law
- **Zero Amnesia / Zero Jump**: The final frame of Episode N (02:00 or 02:20) is the direct kinetic launchpad for the opening frame of Episode N+1 (00:00).
- **Entity State Preservation**: If Character A is bleeding from the knee, wearing torn dust-stained linen, and holding a scorched fragment of a receipt at the end of Episode N, they MUST enter Episode N+1 with the exact same physical wound, fabric condition, and handheld prop.
- **Spatial & Lighting Match**: If Episode N ends in pre-dawn amber warmth torch smoke, Episode N+1 cannot open in sunny midday unless a deliberate, explicit narrative time-skip is established with an environmental establishing shot and physical fatigue indicators.

### 2. Living Realism & Biological Continuum (Food, Sleep, Fatigue)
- **Diurnal Clock Tracking**: Explicitly track time across a 24h–48h window: Dawn (amber warmth) ➔ Midday Sun (cool daylight) ➔ Dusk (amber warmth) ➔ Night (amber warmth / cool daylight).
- **Nutritional & Water Depletion**: Characters cannot run indefinitely on adrenaline. Scripts must track nourishment (barley bread, dried figs, lentils, well-water), bodily hunger, throat dryness, and sweat.
- **Sleep Deprivation & Physical Toll**: Sleeplessness causes red-rimmed eyes, slower reactions, and trembling artisan hands. These micro-biomechanics must be baked into prompts.

### 3. Episodic Dynamic Arc (Ban on Template Monotony)
- A multi-episode series is NOT five identical short films pasted together.
- **BANNED**: Using the identical 8-beat micro-arc (Hook ➔ Escalation ➔ Climax ➔ Resolution) in every single episode.
- **REQUIRED**: Distinct episodic functions:
  - **Act 1 (Inception / Trap)**: Wound established, contract signed, threat unresolved (Open Loop).
  - **Act 2 Early (Complication / Raid)**: Covert betrayal, midnight raid, devastating physical loss.
  - **Act 2 Late (Siege / Courtroom)**: Mob pressure, public shaming, legal standoff, frozen tension.
  - **Act 3 (The Grand Climax & Ego Death)**: Full 8-beat forensic showdown, truth bombs, villain's collapse.
  - **Act 4 (Restorative Grace & Catharsis)**: Unexpected mercy, communal feast, quiet spiritual elevation.

- **Slow Creeping Push-In**: Accelerates heart rate as truth surfaces (`Slow dramatic push-in on character's unblinking eyes`).

- **Snap Whip-Pan Cut**: Explosive transition between arguing foes (`Rapid whip-pan transition from accuser to unyielding defender`).

- **Vertical Crane-Down**: Descends from cavernous ceiling into intimate choker (`Graceful crane descent from high chandelier into intimate choker`).

### § 4. 3D Spatial Staging & Protective Sanctuary Geometry for Minors
1. **The Protective Sanctuary Zone (Vùng An Toàn Bất Khả Xâm Phạm):**
   - In any scene involving a child or infant, spatial staging must establish a concrete architectural Sanctuary Zone:
     * *Direct Presence:* A sturdy pine crib (`noi_go`), soft bedding on a low platform bed, or the secure embrace of a nurturing parent.
     * *Protective Buffer:* Maintain a minimum 2-meter physical buffer between any adult interpersonal confrontation and the child sanctuary zone. The child is NEVER staged between arguing adults or in the trajectory of aggressive movement.
2. **Camera Blocking & Eyeline Respect:**
   - In wholesome scenes with children, framing must use gentle Eye-Level, Shoulder-Level, or soft High-Angle looking down with maternal/paternal warmth.
   - Strictly prohibit aggressive, looming low-angle dominance, Dutch angles, or rapid handheld shakes over minors. Use smooth Slow Push-In or Static Lock-off with serene depth of field.
3. **The Ghost Minor Spatial Staging (In Conflict Scenes):**
   - When adult characters engage in emotional breakdown, crying, or intense arguments, stage the crib or child prop in the foreground/background as a silent moral anchor.
   - The camera blocks the adult subjects while keeping the crib structure stationary and undisturbed. The minor entity is never directly rendered inside the prompt, preserving 100% policy immunity and allowing the full force of adult drama to resonate.
