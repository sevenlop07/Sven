# 2b — CHARACTER LINES (The Canonical Cast System for Films & Series)

> **What this is**: the single, short rulebook for **who is in the story and how each character line travels across a film or series**. Pillar 2 (`2-casting-worldbuilding-specification.md`) keeps the long profile templates, the 20 satellite archetypes and the model-sheet specs; Pillar 1 keeps the narrative catalogs. When they disagree with this file on **role names, cast topology, arcs or introductions**, this file wins. Numbers come from `0c-canonical-numbers.md` (§4, §6).
> **Read**: at Stage 0 (cast table), Stage 1 (cast, arc map) and Stage 3 (episode cast line, arc-map update).

---

## §0 — ROLE VOCABULARY (ONE MEANING PER LABEL)

| Label | Always means | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | the protagonist — the character whose choices drive the story | Co-leads are `A1a` + `A1b` (romance, buddy stories). An ensemble lead group is `E1…E5` (sitcom). |
| **A2** | the **antagonistic force** — always the opposition, never an ally, partner or spouse on A1's side | A person (villain, rival, opposing relative), a **hidden** person (thriller culprit: the writer knows, the viewer does not), or a non-person force (illness, time, a haunting, a system). A non-person A2 still needs a human face in scenes (see §2). |
| **B** | named satellites | Recorded as `B# · B-XX code (§1) · side: A1 / A2 / cross`. |
| **C** | the chorus — unnamed society, crowd, colleagues, online opinion | Never on a model sheet. |

> Any older table that calls an ally or spouse "A2", or sorts the cast as "Phe A / Phe B", is a legacy label: translate it with this vocabulary.

## §2 — CAST TOPOLOGY BY GENRE

The **dual-relationship mandate** (every satellite tied to both A1 and A2) applies **only when A2 is a person**. Each genre uses its own topology:

| Genre | Leads | A2 | Satellites | Chorus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Family drama | A1 | an opposing relative ("love pointed the wrong way") | family members, each tied to both sides | neighbours, relatives, community |
| Microdrama | A1 (hidden status) | a **ladder** of oppressors, rising in power, + the main oppressor | ally, betrayer, a witness who holds proof | the mocking crowd that later turns |
| Sitcom | ensemble `E1–E5`, each with one fixed flaw and want | none (or a recurring rival) | 1–2 recurring guests | customers, neighbours |
| Thriller | A1 investigator | the **hidden** culprit + 2–3 suspects (one is the culprit) | partner, informant, a trusted face who may turn | witnesses, press |
| Action | A1 | the boss + an **opponent ladder** (each exposes one weakness of A1) | mentor, ally, traitor | civilians in danger (never minors on screen in danger) |
| Healing | A1 | none — life, time, illness or loss is the obstacle | the people who enter A1's routine | the neighbourhood |
| Horror | A1 (+ companions) | the entity + a human who keeps the secret | believer, sceptic, victim of the past | villagers, family |
| Romance | co-leads `A1a + A1b` (each with their own Lie) | the obstacle: a rival, a family, a contract, a secret — often plus each lover's fear | best friends (confidants), the rival | colleagues, family |
| Fantasy | A1 (with an edge and its price) | the power holder of the world + the world's law | mentor, rival, a relic keeper | court, sect, villagers |
| Sci-fi | A1 | the system and those who run it | insider, victim of the system, ally | citizens |

## §3 — PROTAGONIST ARCHETYPES & ARC TYPES

Pillar 2 Part II lists five A1 archetypes (A1-01…A1-05), all enduring and virtuous. Add these active, flawed archetypes — the flaw must cause the crisis (doctrine §1.1):

| Code | Archetype | The flaw that drives the plot | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1-06 | The Schemer | believes control and manipulation keep people safe | drama, thriller, microdrama |
| A1-07 | The Climber | trades relationships for status | drama, microdrama |
| A1-08 | The Avenger | revenge before everything, including the people it hurts | microdrama, action, fantasy |
| A1-09 | The Liar | one lie that must be protected with bigger ones | sitcom, romance, drama |
| A1-10 | The Reluctant Hero | avoids responsibility until it is too late | action, fantasy, sci-fi |
| A1-11 | The Lovable Fool | overconfidence and a short memory | sitcom, romance |
| A1-12 | The Obsessed Investigator | needs to be right more than to be safe | thriller, horror |
| A1-13 | The Frozen Mourner | refuses to let go of a loss | healing, horror, romance |

- **Age**: adult leads are any age `≥ 18`; under-18 characters follow the Minor Protection Protocol (`workflow.md` §2.7). The 25–70 range in the Pillar 2 template is not a limit.
- **Vocation**: arena-true and giving leverage in the conflict; never the same vocation set across projects (`workflow.md` §5.1).

**Arc types** (declare one per principal):

| Arc | Shape | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Positive change | Lie ➔ crisis ➔ truth accepted | the default |
| Steadfast (flat) | the character already holds the truth and changes the world around them | mentors, healing leads, some heroes |
| Corrupt then redeem | falls into the Lie deeper, then turns at a cost | avengers, climbers |
| Fall (negative) | the Lie wins | only when `ending_mode` is bittersweet or tragic, or for A2 / satellites |

## §4 — SERIES ARC POINTS (EVERY PRINCIPAL)

For A1, A2 and every B with `≥ 3` episodes, fix five points at Stage 1:

| Point | Where | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Start | Act I | What do they want, and what Lie protects them? |
| First crack | end of Act IIA | What event first threatens the Lie? |
| Midpoint turn | Act IIB | What do they learn or lose that changes their tactic? |
| Bottom | Act IIIA | What does the Lie cost them at its worst? |
| End | Finale | What do they give up, and what is the closing image of their line? |

A2's points run in parallel: their plan advances, cracks, turns, overreaches and fails — the viewer should understand each step from A2's side.

## §5 — CHARACTER ARC MAP (EPISODES × CHARACTERS)

Locked at Stage 1 (`CP1`) for the whole series and **updated at the end of every episode** (Stage 3 Phần 5). One row per named character, one cell per episode:

```text
| Nhân vật (vai · mã) | Tập 1 | Tập 2 | … | Tập N |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lan (A1 · A1-09) | Muốn: … · Biết: … · Quan hệ: … · Lời nói dối: nguyên | … | … | … |
| Minh (A2) | Kế hoạch: bước 1 · Áp lực lên A1: … | … | … | … |
| Hưng (B1 · B-02 · cross) | Xuất hiện: có · Cung riêng: bắt đầu | vắng (đang ở viện — đã cài ở Tập 1) | … | … |
```

Each cell records: **Want** · **Knows** (what they know that matters) · **Relationship state** (with A1 / A2) · **Lie status** (intact / cracked / broken / accepted truth) · present or absent (with the reason).

**Rules**
1. **A2 pressures every episode** — on screen or through a visible consequence.
2. **No named character is absent more than 2 consecutive episodes** without a planted reason; when they return, they return changed.
3. **Every B with `≥ 3` episodes has a mini-arc** (start ➔ turn ➔ end) and at least one scene where they choose something on their own.
4. **A1 is in most scenes of every episode** (default `≥ 60%`) unless the episode is a declared POV switch (`BREAK:` line).
5. **Knowledge consistency**: nobody reacts to information their cell says they do not know (pairs with the "Ai biết gì" ledger).

## §6 — INTRODUCING CHARACTERS

1. **How many**: Episode 1 introduces at most `3–4` named characters on screen; later episodes introduce at most `1–2` new named characters (`0c` §4). An ensemble pilot may break this with a `BREAK:` line.
2. **Introduce by action**: the first shot of a character shows what they want and their flaw in one physical action (the schemer rearranging someone else's papers; the fool dropping the cake and smiling).
3. **Make name and relation clear inside the first scene** — through how others address them (kinship pronouns, titles) or a visual cue, never through a caption.
4. **Never introduce two new named characters in the same scene** unless one of them is already known by reputation.
5. **Re-introduce after absence**: a returning character gets a visual signature (their object, sound or gesture) in the first 3 seconds of their return.
6. **Introduction shot**: use `3c` §11 *Reveal of a character* (feet or back first, face last) only for characters who matter for the season.

## §7 — THE CHORUS BY GENRE

- The "mockery ➔ shame ➔ celebration" mob arc (Pillar 2 STEP 4) is for **microdrama and public-injustice drama**.
- Sitcom chorus = comic reactions; thriller = unreliable witnesses; horror = villagers who know and won't say; healing = a warm neighbourhood; romance = colleagues and family who comment on the couple.
- Group scenes follow `3c` §11 (geography first, then only the 1–2 people in conflict act).
