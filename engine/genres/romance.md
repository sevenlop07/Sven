## 1. The Romance Equation

$$\text{Romance} = \text{Attraction} \times \text{A Real Obstacle Inside Each Lover} + \text{Rising Cost of Admitting It}$$

1. **Two protagonists, two Lies** — each lover carries a Wound and a Lie that the other threatens (Doctrine §8.4). The external obstacle (family, contract, class, distance, rival) only works because it presses on those Lies.
2. **Proximity engine** — the premise forces them together (shared contract, shared house, shared case) so the obstacle is tested scene after scene.
3. **Every episode moves the distance** — measure the emotional distance between the lovers at the start and end of each episode; it must change (closer or broken), never stay flat.
4. **The declaration is earned** — the confession or grand gesture comes when admitting love costs the most (Doctrine §8.8).

## 2. Romance Mechanics (screenplay side)

- **Subtext over statement**: lovers never say "I like you" until it costs; they argue about the umbrella, the late reply, the borrowed jacket (Doctrine §8.9).
- **Near-miss ladder**: almost-touch ➔ interrupted confession ➔ one real moment ➔ cold water caused by a plant (Doctrine §9.8). Never two near-misses of the same kind in a row.
- **Misunderstanding discipline**: obey the 6 conditions and the one-act lifespan of Doctrine §9.5; resolve it by a character choosing to expose themselves.
- **Consent and respect**: romantic tension never comes from coercion, stalking or possessiveness presented as love.
- **Tier-1 prop**: a shared object that changes meaning (the contract, the ring, the umbrella, the voice message) — the Echo prop of the ending.

## 3. Episode Shape (default sketch — flex per 0c §4)

| Phase | Function |
|---|---|
| Open (15–20%) | The two in friction or forced proximity (hook on a body/expression detail) |
| Develop (40–50%) | Banter with subtext; a private moment shows what each hides; the obstacle tightens |
| Climax (20–25%) | A choice between the lover and the Lie; a near-confession or a sacrifice |
| Close (10%) | The distance has changed; an interruption, a rival or a revealed clause leaves the question open |

## 4. Pre-Flight Additions
1. What is the emotional distance between the lovers at the start and end of this episode?
2. Does each lover act from their own Lie, not only from the external obstacle?
3. Would one honest sentence end the conflict? If yes, what makes that sentence costly?
4. Is every romantic beat free of coercion and of minors in romantic physical content?
