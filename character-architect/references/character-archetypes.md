# Character Archetypes — Global 35+ Storytelling
## Framework: Tier A / B1 / B2 / C

Every character in a short story exists within a precise functional relationship to the protagonist's emotional journey. This document defines the archetypes, visual anchor specifications, and profile templates for all four tiers.

---

## CORE PRINCIPLES
1. **Flawed Humanity**: Characters are not types — they are living contradictions.
2. **Specific Physicality**: Postures, scars, gait, and sensory textures define characters more than descriptive adjectives.
3. **No Uncontested Rightness**: Every antagonist must have a point where they are objectively correct.
4. **Wounds Drive Action**: The wound is not backstory — it is the lens through which every decision in the present is made.

---

## TIER A — PROTAGONIST

**The story revolves around this character.** Every plot beat, scene, and emotional pivot serves their internal transformation or steadfast moral choice.

**Mandatory Requirements Before Writing:**
- Specific wound (tangible incident, date/timeline, persons involved — not vague sadness).
- Want $\neq$ Need (if identical, no dramatic tension exists).
- 1 UNIQUE MARK locked in the Visual Anchor.
- 1 named internal contradiction.

---

### A-01 | The Quiet Carrier
**Age**: 50–65 | **Gender**: Any

**Core**: Has carried a profound weight — grief, unconfessed guilt, a family secret — for so long that it has become their physical posture. They have learned to function seamlessly around this gravity.

**Wound**: The exact moment they chose silence over confrontation, and lived with the silent aftermath ever since.

**Want** (Surface): To keep the peace — avoid stirring old waters, avoid confrontation.

**Need** (Truth): To speak the buried truth aloud before no one remains to hear it.

**Internal Contradiction**: Surprisingly dry, observational humor. Humor is their survival mechanism and their impenetrable armor.

**Observable Habits**: Turns their wristwatch repeatedly when anxious (or stopped wearing a watch on a specific date). Always sits with a line of sight to the door. Never calls ahead — simply arrives.

**Best Paired With**: B1-01 (Mirror That Shows Too Much), B2-01 (The Accidental Mirror).

**Typical Occupations**: Retired machinist, head librarian, long-haul route driver, school building superintendent.

---

### A-02 | The Provider Who Forgot Themselves
**Age**: 48–60 | **Gender**: Any, often female

**Core**: Has spent decades organizing, shielding, and sustaining everyone else — family, workplace, community. Faces life where they are no longer centrally needed.

**Wound**: Identity built entirely on being indispensable. When the crisis passes — who are they?

**Want**: Rest, appreciation, acknowledged sacrifice.

**Need**: To learn how to exist for their own soul, not merely as a service to others.

**Internal Contradiction**: Ferociously decisive when backed into a corner. Decades of managing others' chaos created immense capability — they have simply never directed it toward their own life.

**Observable Habits**: Anticipates others' physical needs before they speak. Maintains an exhaustive mental checklist. Drinks cold coffee because they forget to drink it while hot.

**Best Paired With**: B1-04 (The Comfortable Life), B2-03 (The Unexpected Friend).

**Typical Occupations**: Charge nurse, high school teacher, family diner owner, hospice caregiver.

---

### A-03 | The Man Who Never Asked for Help
**Age**: 55–68 | **Gender**: Male

**Core**: Self-reliance as religion. Mistook emotional silence for strength for thirty years. Now faces a reality where silence cannot mend the rupture.

**Wound**: Conditioned by an unyielding father or early failure that asking for assistance is unpardonable weakness.

**Want**: To fix the disaster single-handedly without anyone noticing the cracks.

**Need**: To discover that accepting grace and assistance is the ultimate act of courage.

**Internal Contradiction**: Disarming gentleness with animals or small children that he has zero vocabulary to explain.

**Observable Habits**: Fixes broken objects immediately without asking. Never initiates physical affection, but leans in when embraced. Keeps a pocket tool at all times even in retirement.

**Best Paired With**: B2-02 (The Persistent Truth-Teller), B1-05 (The Well-Meaning Saboteur).

**Typical Occupations**: Independent contractor, diesel mechanic, orchardist, retired fire captain.

---

### A-04 | The Late Bloomer
**Age**: 46–58 | **Gender**: Any

**Core**: Attempting or claiming an authentic path for the first time at an age when society expects quiet coasting. Terrifying, quiet revolution.

**Wound**: Spent the first half of life fulfilling everyone else's expectations.

**Want**: To prove that time has not run out.

**Need**: To understand that true courage is acting despite genuine fear, not in the absence of it.

**Internal Contradiction**: Privately overwhelmed by imposter syndrome while projecting steady resolve.

**Observable Habits**: Begins profound sentences and stops midway. Keeps a hidden journal. Sets the morning alarm 15 minutes early to lie awake thinking.

**Best Paired With**: B1-03 (The Internal Antagonist), B2-02 (The Persistent Truth-Teller).

---

### A-05 | The One Who Left and Came Back
**Age**: 45–60 | **Gender**: Any

**Core**: Has returned to the original landscape — hometown, family estate, abandoned craft — and must face both who they were when they fled and who they became away.

**Wound**: The departure itself — was it necessary survival or selfish abandonment?

**Want**: To be welcomed back smoothly as though the gap never occurred.

**Need**: To acknowledge the rupture, accept accountability, and discover if forgiveness is possible.

**Internal Contradiction**: Changed profoundly by what they ran toward, yet haunted by what they abandoned.

**Observable Habits**: Notices physical changes in the landscape before recognizing familiar faces. Still knows all backroads instinctively.

**Best Paired With**: B1-02 (The One Who Was Wronged), B2-01 (The Accidental Mirror).

## TIER B — SECONDARY CAST

Tier B consists of **B1 (Antagonist / Pressure Source)** and **B2 (Companion / Catalyst)**. Both require psychological depth.

---

### B1 — ANTAGONIST (The Pressure Source)

**Core Principle**: In mature storytelling, the antagonist is rarely an evil caricature. They are the force that renders the protagonist's wound unavoidable. **THEY MUST BE RIGHT ABOUT SOMETHING.**

#### B1-01 | The Mirror That Shows Too Much
**Core**: Speaks the precise unvarnished truth the protagonist evades — without malice, but with devastating accuracy.
**Where They Are Right**: Their diagnosis of the protagonist's flaw is accurate.
**Blind Spot**: Confuses harsh truth with compassion; lacks empathy in delivery.
**Internal Contradiction**: Demands absolute truth from others while concealing their own private failure.

#### B1-02 | The One Who Was Wronged
**Core**: Legitimately suffered due to the protagonist's past failure or departure.
**Where They Are Right**: Their grievance is genuine and earned.
**Blind Spot**: Has weaponized victimhood into a permanent identity that blocks healing.
**Internal Contradiction**: Secretly misses the bond more than they crave retribution.

#### B1-03 | The Internal Antagonist (Manifested Externally)
**Core**: A family member or peer who embodies the exact compromise or cynicism the protagonist fears becoming.
**Where They Are Right**: Practical compromises are necessary to survive in an unyielding world.
**Blind Spot**: Believes idealism is purely naive folly.

#### B1-04 | The Comfortable Life
**Core**: Offers easy safety, routine, and comfortable stagnation that would suffocate the protagonist's soul.
**Where They Are Right**: Stability and security have immense real value.
**Blind Spot**: Does not realize that safety without purpose is a slow death.

#### B1-05 | The Well-Meaning Saboteur
**Core**: Deeply loves the protagonist but attempts to protect them by suffocating their growth or hiding critical truth.
**Where They Are Right**: Their motivation stems from genuine protective love.
**Blind Spot**: Infantilizes the protagonist and denies them agency.

---

### B2 — COMPANION / CATALYST (The Anchor)

**Core Principle**: B2 does not solve the story for the protagonist. They create a safe, grounded space where truth can surface.

#### B2-01 | The Accidental Mirror
**Core**: Unintentionally reflects the protagonist's dilemma through their own unrelated life struggles.
**Function**: Allows protagonist to observe their own flaw from a detached third-person perspective.
**Internal Contradiction**: Completely unaware of the profound impact they have on the protagonist.

#### B2-02 | The Persistent Truth-Teller
**Core**: Older or weathered companion who has already survived the trial the protagonist faces.
**Function**: Anchors wisdom without lecturing; provides living proof that survival is possible.
**Internal Contradiction**: Carries their own quiet regrets, making their guidance authentic and humble.

#### B2-03 | The Unexpected Friend
**Core**: From an entirely different generation, social background, or culture. Zero investment in who the protagonist used to be.
**Function**: Grants the protagonist permission to explore a new identity without historical baggage.

#### B2-04 | The Doubter (As Foil)
**Core**: Sibling, peer, or longtime friend who made the opposite life choice.
**Function**: Externalizes the protagonist's doubts into concrete dialogue.
**Internal Contradiction**: Doubts out of deep protective love, not jealousy.

---

### B3 ⭐ — EMOTIONAL ORIGIN ANCHOR (The Sacred Wound / Mỏ Neo Cội Nguồn Cảm Xúc)

**Core Principle**: Tier B3 is the biological, familial, or debt-of-gratitude anchor who represents the origin of the protagonist's core emotional wound. Even if deceased or far away, their memory, past sacrifice, and sacred echo props drive the story across flashbacks, climaxes, and resolutions.

#### B3-01 | The Sacrificial Mother / Father (Cội Nguồn Hy Sinh)
- **Core**: Borne extreme physical hardship (poverty, illness, cold, labor) to give the protagonist a chance at life.
- **Dynamic with Tier A**: Protagonist spent decades chasing wealth/power under the delusion that "money equals repaid love", only to realize B3 only ever wanted their presence and safety.
- **Sacred Prop Anchor**: Hand-crafted coarse hemp garments, worn prayer beads, unopened letters, handcrafted wooden toys.

#### B3-02 | The Unspoken Father / Strict Anchor (Tình Cha Trầm Lặng)
- **Core**: Love expressed through silent labor, endurance, and harsh discipline rather than verbal warmth.
- **Dynamic with Tier A**: Protagonist fled to escape his shadow, carrying an inherited fear of failure until realizing his father's harshness was armor against a brutal world.

#### B3-03 | The Late Spouse / Lost Heart (Mảnh Vỡ Hôn Nhân / Tình Cũ)
- **Core**: The partner who passed away or separated, leaving an unfinished promise or unhealed grief.
- **Dynamic with Tier A**: Protagonist trapped in gilded mourning, unable to love again until completing a sacred threshold gesture of release.

#### B3-04 | The Dependent Child / Innocent Sibling (Trách Nhiệm Thiêng Liêng)
- **Core**: The vulnerable dependent whose future the protagonist swore to protect.
- **Dynamic with Tier A**: Serves as the ultimate moral compass whenever the protagonist is tempted by cynical commercial shortcuts.

---

### B4+ — RECURRING ALLIES & SUB-STAKEHOLDERS (Đồng Minh Lặp Lại $\ge 2$ Cảnh)

#### B4-01 | The Loyal Factor / Second-in-Command (Phó Tướng / Quản Gia)
- **Function**: Manages daily logistics; reflects how the protagonist treats those under their economic power.

#### B4-02 | The Inheritor / Young Apprentice (Học Trò / Người Kế Thừa)
- **Function**: Represents the next generation watching the protagonist's moral choices; becomes the vessel for the protagonist's transformed wisdom in Act III.

## TIER C — BACKGROUND CROWD & WITNESS CAST (QUẦN CHÚNG & NHÂN VẬT NỀN)

**Core Principle**: Tier C is a **collective group / background category** (NOT a single isolated individual character). Tier C represents the living community, working crowds, passing travelers, and episodic witnesses that ground the story in physical reality and prove the protagonist does not live in an empty vacuum.

### C-01 | Working Crowds & Atmospheric Groups (Tập Thể Quần Chúng)
- **Examples**: Caravan drivers and grooms (`CUE-17`), armed caravan guards (`CUE-28`), bustling bazaar merchants (`CUE-05`), trading floor crowds (`CUE-39`), village families and children receiving relief (`CUE-107`).
- **Function**: Manifests the socio-economic scale of the world and visually reflects societal reaction or collective community transformation.

### C-02 | The Accidental Oracle / Passing Witness (Nhân Chứng Thoáng Qua)
- **Examples**: The wandering Buddhist monk delivering a scroll (`CUE-33`), the elderly innkeeper, the passing traveler asking questions.
- **Function**: Utters an ordinary, casual observation or executes a physical action that strikes the protagonist with double thematic meaning.

### C-03 | Visual Rules & 4-Shield Anti-Corruption Protocol for Tier C:
- **Zero Reference Sheets**: Tier C is **STRICTLY EXCLUDED from 4-View Character Reference Model Sheets** (Model Sheets are strictly reserved for named Tier A and Tier B characters appearing in $\ge 2$ scenes).
- **Scene Prompt Integration**: In scene prompts, describe Tier C collectively through era-appropriate sensory clothing and physical actions without direct camera eye contact (`caravan drivers in coarse wool cloaks leading draft horses`, `poor mountain villagers gathering with wooden bowls around open wagons`).

## AGE-SPECIFIC PSYCHOLOGICAL LAYERS

### Characters in their 40s:
- Still striving to prove self-worth — now to themselves rather than society.
- Acute awareness that the first half of life has concluded.
- Physical body begins requiring conscious maintenance.
- Children (if any) transitioning into independent individuals.

### Characters in their 50s:
- Calcification of habit — routines practiced for 25 years feel immutable.
- Losses arrive in waves: aging parents, peers, former ambitions.
- Relationships either deepen into profound grace or fracture irreparably.
- Career trajectory reaches culmination or quiet plateau.

### Characters in their 60s+:
- Mortality shifts from intellectual concept to immediate physical reality.
- The Great Inventory: Did I live with genuine integrity?
- Legacy consciousness — what enduring truth will outlast me?
- Remarkable liberation from superficial social anxieties that plagued earlier decades.
