# STORY RULES — Universal Cinematic Storytelling Framework

> This is a universal storytelling ruleset applicable to all genres — not limited to karma, spiritual, or any specific setting.
> Applies to: Short Film (TikTok/Reels), Multi-Episode Series, AI Video Scripts.

---

## 🎬 5 PILLARS OF CINEMATIC DEPTH (HOLLYWOOD CINEMATIC DEPTH PROTOCOL)

**MANDATORY SYSTEM-WIDE** — Every script MUST follow these 5 pillars, eliminating the instinct to write flat, one-dimensional narratives:

1. **Backstory Trauma**:
   - Character profile MUST explicitly state `Backstory Trauma` — a wound, loss, or trauma that shapes behavior.
   - Every negative behavior in Setup MUST be an unconscious reaction to protect or escape past trauma (Trauma Trigger).

2. **Subtext (Visual Metaphor & Hidden Meaning)**:
   - No action is meaningless. Every prop and micro-gesture carries a message beneath the surface.

3. **Self-Fulfilling Causality**:
   - Consequences do not come from one-directional violent punishment — they arise from **the character's own panic, guilt, and self-imposed psychological paralysis** — psychology transferred to the body.

4. **Subject-Centric Micro-Biomechanics**:
   - 100% of scene description sentences MUST follow this syntax: `[Subject] + [Primary action] + [Expressive micro-biomechanics: neck vein tensing, pupil contracting, breath rhythm, lips tightening] + [Prop/setting interaction]`. FORBIDDEN: passive, vague constructions.

5. **Concrete Visual Anchoring**:
   - 100% of emotions, lessons, and motivations must be encoded as observable specific actions / objects / light. FORBIDDEN: captions, explanatory text overlaid on image.

---

### GENRE — Impact on Story

Genre defines the **emotional framework and conflict** of the entire narrative:

| Genre | Story Impact |
|---|---|
| Billionaire Romance / CEO Workplace | Power imbalance is the primary conflict source — class gap, ambition vs. love |
| Revenge Drama | Character arc must have a turning point — from revenge → realizing the cost |
| SEA Family Drama | Conflict from within the family — duty, expectations, sacrifice |
| Thriller Mystery | Every Beat must raise tension — no "neutral" Beat |
| J-Drama Healing | Slow burn — emotion arc builds slowly, catharsis at Beat 7-8 |
| Survival Adventure | High physical stakes — environment is the third character |
| Light Comedy | Subtext must still exist — funny but not empty |

**Rule:** Genre chosen → STEP 0 only generates ideas fitting that genre. Do not mix conflicting genres.

---

### BACKGROUND — Impact on World-Building

Background is the **anchor environment** — every scene must be consistent with this setting:

| Background | Story Implication |
|---|---|
| Modern City | Visual: neon/concrete/crowd — loneliness in a crowd is natural subtext |
| Small Town | Tight space → inescapable conflict → forced confrontation |
| Luxury Mansion | Material objects mask real emotion — props are the language of power |
| Hospital / Courtroom | High-stakes setting → every action carries major consequences |
| Supernatural Realm | Different physical rules — must be established in Beat 1-2 |
| Mafia Underworld | Trust is a weapon — every character hides an agenda |

**Rule:** Background chosen in 1A → Environment Profile (1D) must be built around it. Initial Setting Declaration must be consistent.

---

### TROPE — Impact on Narrative Structure

Trope defines **dramatic structure and twist rhythm**:

| Trope | Beat Impact |
|---|---|
| Hidden Identity | Twist at Beat 5-6 — character is exposed. Next beat: fallout |
| Enemies to Lovers | Slow escalation — tension builds gradually to Beat 6 |
| Revenge Comeback | Beat 1-4: disadvantage / Beat 5-7: rise / Beat 8: reckoning |
| Second Chance | Flashback allowed at Beat 2-3 to anchor emotion |
| Redemption Arc | Beat 8 MUST be catharsis — no ambiguous ending |
| Found Family | Supporting character carries equal emotional weight to Character A |
| Fake Relationship | Subtext must be clearer than dialogue — audience must see truth before character does |
| Time Travel / Rebirth | Must establish "rules" of the trope at Beat 1 — no mid-story explanation |

**Rule — Stack maximum 2 tropes:**
- 2 chosen tropes must be **compatible** — no conflicting twist rhythms
- Valid combos: `Redemption Arc + Found Family` / `Enemies to Lovers + Fake Relationship`
- Invalid combos: `Hidden Identity + Amnesia` (2 twists simultaneously → diluted)

**Anti-Repetition:** Used tropes are logged in `history-registry.json` — do not repeat within the same series.

## § BEAT MATRIX — STANDARD 8 BEAT × 15s ARCHITECTURE

> **Core principle:**
> - 1 episode = **8 fixed Beats × 15s** = **2 minutes**
> - 1 Beat = **2 AI Clips** (Clip A: 7s + Clip B: 8s) = **1 complete state change**
> - Emotion Score (1–10) tracks emotional rhythm — MUST have at least 1 beat reaching 9–10

---

### 🎬 FORMAT 1 — SHORT FILM (1 self-contained episode = 2 minutes)

**Characteristic**: 1 complete story arc within 8 Beats × 15s. No cliffhanger — Beat 8 is a still Catharsis.

#### 8-Beat Table — Short Film

| Beat | Phase | Function | Emotion Score | Mandatory Rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **HOOK** | Cognitive Gap + Visual Anchor. NO explanation. | 6–7 / 10 | Visual Anchor must reappear in Beat 2 within first 3s |
| **2** | **SETUP 1** | Open Visual Anchor, introduce character, setting | 6–7 / 10 | Copy HANDOFF STATE from Beat 1 |
| **3** | **SETUP 2** | Plant symbolic prop, Backstory Trauma hint, seed of conflict | 6–7 / 10 | Symbolic prop must appear for the first time |
| **4** | **ESCALATION 1** | Conflict erupts level 1 — stake clearly established | 7–8 / 10 | Chosen conflict type begins to escalate |
| **5** | **ESCALATION 2** | Conflict escalates level 2 — maximum pressure | 8–9 / 10 | No clear way out remains |
| **6** | **TWIST / CLIMAX** | Breaking point — Twist reverses or absolute emotional peak | **9–10 / 10** | Insert-shot MANDATORY. PEAK of entire episode. |
| **7** | **RESOLUTION** | Natural consequences from Twist — character faces outcome | 8–9 / 10 | Use the exact symbolic prop planted in Beat 3 |
| **8** | **AFTERMATH** | Still. Mirrors Beat 1. Catharsis. NO cliffhanger. | 6–7 / 10 | Image from Beat 1 reappears. Positive ending. |

**Standard Short Film Emotion Arc:** `6→6→7→8→9→10→8→7`

> Beat merge rules when needed (short < 2 minutes):
> 1. NEVER cut Beat 1 (Hook) or Beat 6 (Twist/Climax)
> 2. Merge priority: Beat 2↔3 first, then Beat 7↔8
> 3. Symbolic prop is NEVER cut even when merging

---

### 🎞️ FORMAT 2 — MULTI-EPISODE SERIES (N episodes × 2 minutes)

**Characteristic**: Each episode = 8 Beats × 15s = 2 minutes. N episodes form 1 Series Arc divided into 4 Acts. Beat 8 of each episode (except the finale) ends with a **Cliffhanger** instead of Catharsis.

#### 4-Act Distribution by Total Episode Count

```
Total episodes = N (user decides, default: ask)

Act 1 (Setup)      = floor(N × 0.25) episodes, minimum 1
Act 2 (Escalation) = floor(N × 0.25) episodes, minimum 1
Act 3 (Climax)     = floor(N × 0.25) episodes, minimum 1
Act 4 (Resolution) = N − (A1 + A2 + A3) episodes, minimum 1
```

#### Roles of 4 Acts

| Act | Name | Script Duty | Cliffhanger Intensity | Cliffhanger Type |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Setup** | Introduce world, characters, core conflict, plant symbolic prop | 6–7 / 10 | Mystery / Threat |
| **2** | **Escalation** | Raise stakes, test relationships, Midpoint Twist reverses everything | 7–8 / 10 | Betrayal / Revelation |
| **3** | **Climax** | Darkest point, character at rock bottom, all secrets exposed, decision formed | 9–10 / 10 | Loss / Decision |
| **4** | **Resolution** | Final confrontation, catharsis, **positive ending** | — (finale) | NONE |

#### Example — 8-Episode Series

| Episode | Act | Duty | Cliffhanger |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Setup | Introduce protagonist + core conflict | Mystery — C1 |
| 2 | Setup | First stake, situation becomes clearer, catalyst character appears | Threat — C2 |
| 3 | Escalation | First failure, first secret discovered | Betrayal — C3 |
| 4 | Escalation | Midpoint Twist — everything is reversed | Revelation — C4 |
| 5 | Climax | Darkest point, character loses what matters most | Loss — C5 |
| 6 | Climax | All secrets exposed — final decision forms | Decision — C6 |
| 7 | Resolution | Final confrontation, character executes decision | Victory hook — C2 |
| 8 | Resolution | Full Resolution + Catharsis, positive ending | NONE |

#### Beat 8 of Each Episode — 2 Versions

| Episode | Beat 8 Role | Content |
|---|---|---|
| **Mid-series episodes** | **CLIFFHANGER** | Still 10s → Hanging question or action cut right before breaking point |
| **Series finale** | **CATHARSIS + AFTERMATH** | Completely still → Mirrors Beat 1 of Episode 1 → Lesson absorbed through image |

#### 7 Cliffhanger Types

| # | Type | Description | Suitable Act |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Mystery** | Unanswered visual question hanging in the air | Act 1 |
| C2 | **Threat** | New unexpected danger appears at episode end | Act 1, 4 |
| C3 | **Betrayal** | Trusted person revealed as traitor | Act 2 |
| C4 | **Revelation** | Truth reverses the entire picture | Act 2 |
| C5 | **Loss** | Character loses what matters most | Act 3 |
| C6 | **Decision** | Cut right before an irreversible decision moment | Act 3 |
| C7 | **Return** | Character from the past unexpectedly reappears | Act 2–3 |

## § HOOK TECHNIQUES — 12 TYPES & SELECTION TABLE BY PLOT

> 📌 **SYSTEM DEFAULT**: Use **Hook #4 — "Frozen Frame" (Suspended Frame / Motion Freeze)** for 100% of scripts, unless user directly requests a change.

### 4 Life-or-Death Elements of the Hook Scene (First 3 Seconds)
1. **Cognitive Gap**: Immediately create a visual question in the audience's brain (*"Why is it like that?"*, *"What's about to happen?"*). DO NOT explain context or cause.
2. **Single Visual Signal Rule**: Do not cram in character/history/context. Only one close-up detail (ECU) or one condensed physical contradiction.
3. **Feel Before Understand (Emotional Impulse First)**: Hit the emotional instinct (Curiosity, Emotion, Tension) in the first 3s before the audience can process logic.
4. **Visual Anchor Bridge**: The Hook scene MUST contain 1 specific physical object/detail so that Scene 2 (Setup) opens with the camera starting from exactly that element.

### 🚫 3 Forbidden Traps When Writing Hook:
- ❌ **Narrative Opener Trap**: Opens with narration "Once upon a time...", "In a place...", "There was a person named..."
- ❌ **Spoiler Trap**: Within 3s already reveals who is good/bad, cause and outcome.
- ❌ **Empty Stillness Trap**: Opening scene has only beautiful scenery/setting with no conflict or suspended movement.


| # | Technique | How to Execute | Suggested Example | SVEN Shot Execution |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Visual Paradox** | 2 logically contradictory images placed side by side | Expensive suit placed next to a meager meal | Wide: 2 worlds in same frame → ECU intersection point |
| 2 | **In medias res** | Opens mid-action/climax, cause not yet clear | A hand snatching something, unclear who is right | Close-up ongoing action → gentle pull back → ECU expression |
| 3 | **Strange object/sign** | ECU of an unusual object, no immediate explanation | A crumpled letter lying at a doorstep | ECU static object → push-in/tilt for more context → object + hand |
| **⭐ 4** | **"Frozen Frame" (DEFAULT)** | Freeze at a suspended action moment | A glass falling mid-air, not yet hitting the ground | Medium suspended action → ultra-slow static → ECU motion point |
| 5 | **Atmospheric Contrast** | Setting evokes feeling opposite to what's about to happen | In a festive space, one figure stands completely still | Wide festive scene → rack focus/pull back → Close-up discordant detail |
| 6 | **Body/Expression Detail** | ECU of unusual eyes/hands/gaze, reason not yet clear | A trembling hand gripping something tight | ECU unusual eyes/hand → push-in hold → ECU another body part confirming |
| 7 | **Out of Place / Disrupted Order** | Image that belongs elsewhere appears in the wrong context | An expensive object lying among garbage | Medium/Wide object out of place → tilt/pan hints more → ECU wrong contact point |
| 8 | **Overlapping Time Traces** | Evokes both "before" and "after" simultaneously | Old and new marks on the same surface | Close-up old+new marks together → pull back → ECU intersection point |
| 9 | **Consequence First / Effect Before Cause** | Shows the consequence scene before flashing back | An abandoned room, cause unknown | ECU faded object → slow pull back revealing context → tracking shot |
| 10 | **Scale Contrast** | Contrast between human smallness and massive scale | Tiny character facing a vast space/structure | Extreme Wide → Low angle pan |
| 11 | **Diegetic Acoustic Stimulus** | Hits the hearing directly with hyper-realistic ambient sound | A shattering sound echoing in a silent night | Close-up sound-emitting object → gentle camera shake → ECU reaction |
| 12 | **Cognitive Silence / Glitch** | Chaotic movement suddenly freezes absolutely still for 0.5s | A noisy crowd suddenly stops dead | Tracking fast movement → Instant Freeze Frame 0.5s → ECU eye lock |

### Scoring Framework — Flexible Hook Selection

**Foundational question before selecting:**
> *"What is the first emotion I want the audience to FEEL (not UNDERSTAND) in the first 3 seconds?"*
> → Select the hook that creates exactly that emotion through observable physical images.

#### Trigger Conditions Per Hook

| # | Technique | Activate when plot has... | AVOID when... |
|---|---|---|---|
| **#1** Visual Paradox | Extreme power/status gap; outer appearance concealing inner truth | Plot needs still/quiet tone |
| **#2** In medias res | Ongoing action with unexplained conflict; strong Twist needing misdirection from the start; Structure C (opens with consequence) | Plot needs slow buildup, meditative tone |
| **#3** Strange object/sign | Symbolic prop plays a key role; quiet/anonymous behavior needing physical traces | Symbolic prop not yet clearly established |
| **#4** "Frozen Frame" | Suspended decision moment; acquisitive/greedy behavior needing to emphasize the moment of transgression | Plot needs to create questions about setting (not action) |
| **#5** Atmospheric Contrast | Tragedy hidden in normal appearance; community/group concealing something wrong | Setting already naturally implies danger |
| **#6** Body/Expression Detail | Suppressed emotion at center (silent sacrifice, bearing injustice, hidden fear); vulnerable character needs immediate empathy | Plot needs to keep character identity completely hidden |
| **#7** Out of Place | Object/person appearing against social space norms; mysterious/supernatural element | Purely realistic plot with no mysterious elements |
| **#8** Overlapping Time Traces | Plot has historical depth (years of grudges/bonds); symbolic prop has transformed over time | Story happens within a single day |
| **#9** Consequence First | Narrative Structure C (flashback); conflict measured in years | Linearly progressing plot |
| **#10** Scale Contrast | Character faces something massive (organization, nature, power) | Tight interior setting |
| **#11** Diegetic Acoustic | Peak acoustic tension; dark atmosphere with heavy sound design | Video viewed with sound off by default |
| **#12** Cognitive Silence | Crowd chaos; rapid film rhythm needing a shock freeze | Scene already still |

#### Hook Selection Process (3 Steps — MANDATORY)

**Step 1 — Identify 2–3 most prominent features of the plot:**
- Genre & target emotional tone (haunting / heartbreaking / mysterious / unjust / funny / strange...)
- Chosen Escalation behavior
- Symbolic prop's role in the plot (central / supporting)
- Narrative structure (A–G) — especially Structure C → prioritize #2

**Step 2 — Use "Trigger Conditions" table to evaluate:**
- Hook matching **the most conditions** → **Primary Hook**
- Hook matching **second most** and not violating AVOID column → **Backup Hook**
- Record: `Primary hook: #N | Backup hook: #M | Reason: [1 sentence tied to specific feature]`

**Step 3 — Anti-repetition check:**
- Scan entire session — if primary hook was used → use backup hook
- Add to metadata: `HOOK TECHNIQUE: #N — [technique name] | Reason: [1 sentence]`

### Mandatory Rules When Writing Hook

- DO NOT introduce full name/identity, DO NOT explain setting, DO NOT state reasons — only suggest through image
- AVOID narrative opener style ("one day", "once upon a time") — that is SETUP voice
- Use specific cinematic language (shot scale: close/extreme close/wide; camera movement: pan, tracking, slow zoom...)
- May combine 2 techniques in 1 Hook scene if reasonable, as long as no more than 1 core piece of information

### HOOK-SETUP BRIDGE — Mandatory Cognitive Bridge

> **Core problem:** Hook deliberately hides all information → Scene 2 (Setup) begins storytelling → without a shared visual element, viewers experience **cognitive discontinuity** — failing to understand the 2 scenes belong to the same story.

**MANDATORY RULE:** Every Hook scene MUST contain at least 1 **Visual Anchor** — a specific, observable visual element that will **reappear within the first 3 seconds of Scene 2 (Setup)**.

**5 Bridge Types (priority from top to bottom):**

| # | Type | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Object Bridge** | Symbolic object appears in Hook (ECU, decontextualized) → reappears in Scene 2 in character's hand/nearby | Hook: a key lying on the ground → Scene 2: character holding that key |
| 2 | **Person Bridge** | Body detail/clothing revealed in Hook → full body revealed in Scene 2 | Hook: a trembling hand gripping a shirt hem → Scene 2: full figure wearing that shirt |
| 3 | **Spatial Bridge** | Hook shows one fragment of space (ECU/CU) → Scene 2 expands same space (Wide) | Hook: burn mark on the floor → Scene 2: pull back reveals the entire room |
| 4 | **Reverse Angle** | Same object/space, different angle | Hook: front of object → Scene 2: back of object, pull back reveals character |
| 5 | **Atmospheric** | Same atmosphere (sound, light) continues unbroken | Weakest — **use combined** with types 1-4 only, NEVER standalone |

**Add to script metadata:**
`HOOK→SETUP BRIDGE: [Bridge type] — [specific element from Hook] → [how it reappears in Scene 2]`

**Mandatory check:**
- [ ] Does the visual anchor from Hook reappear within the first 3 seconds of Scene 2?
- [ ] Is the visual anchor described with enough physical detail in both scenes (not just named)?
- [ ] Does Scene 2 NOT reference Scene 1 ("same as Hook", "as seen before") — rewritten with independent detail?

- **Line A (Protagonist)**: Continuous English paragraph, max ~120 words, order: Name → Age → Gender → Height/Size → Skin/Ethnicity → Build → Hair → Facial features (observable behaviors) → Clothing & original props + **Backstory Trauma** (internal note)
- **Line B (Catalyst/Antagonist)**: Same template as Line A
- **Line C (Supporting/Environment)**: 30–40 words, 1–2 sentences [Role] + [1 distinctive appearance] + [1 repeated action]

### Relationship Dynamic

> Declare Line A ↔ Line B relationship in metadata: `RELATIONSHIP: [Type] — [1-sentence description]`

|---|---|---|

### Emotion Physics — ZERO abstract emotion words

| Emotion (DO NOT USE) | Replacement Mechanics (USE THIS) |
|---|---|
| `fearful` / `scared` | Pupils contracting, both shoulders hunching tensed, rapid breaths through parted lips |
| `angry` / `furious` | Temple vein visibly pulsing, jaw locked tight, eyes blazing with fixed intensity |
| `sad` / `sorrowful` | Trembling hands reaching into empty air, a tear rolling through mud-streaked cheek |
| `desperate` / `pleading` | Head bowing down, knees touching floor, open palm not yet reaching the other person |
| `shocked` / `stunned` | Step frozen mid-stride, held object released, mouth falling open without sound |
| `relieved` | Shoulders dropping slowly, a long breath out through the nose, jaw muscles releasing |
| `confident` / `proud` | Head tilted slightly back, stride even and long, eyes scanning the entire room without pausing |
| `guilty` / `ashamed` | Eyes cast downward unable to meet gaze, hand squeezing the held object, shoulders drawing inward |
| `lonely` / `isolated` | Wide space but character curled into a corner, arms crossed over the body, eyes staring far without focus |
| `hopeful` | Shoulder muscles relaxing, head leaning slightly forward, fingers loosening their grip |

## § LINE C — GREEK CHORUS (Crowd / Audience / Mass)

> **Definition:** Line C characters are not meaningless extras — they are **the voice of society** looking into the story with incomplete information. Their misjudgment creates **Dramatic Irony** — the most powerful emotion in drama.

### Line C's Narrative Role

| Function | Mechanism | Emotional Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Social Mirror** | Reflects collective bias (money = competent, silence = weakness) | Audience sees injustice — hurts for the protagonist |
| **Unwitting Witness** | Present but lacking full context | Protagonist is judged publicly without being allowed to defend |
| **Contrast Layer** | Line A silently endures / Line C loudly misjudges | Contrast highlights Character A's inner world |
| **Living Texture** | Creates the feeling of a world operating around the story | Setting is not a set — it is the real world |

### Line C's Judgment Principles

1. **They always judge by surface** — no context of Character A/B's inner world
2. **They usually judge wrongly** — criticize the protagonist, praise the antagonist
3. **They don't know they're wrong** — speak naturally, confidently, without malice
4. **Their judgment reflects real social bias** — not villains, just ordinary people

### Designing Line C in the Script

**No individual names.** Describe by group characteristics:
- Estimated number: `1 male vendor ~40`, `3 frightened villagers`, `a loose group of 8`
- Collective emotion/attitude: fearful, gossiping, admiring, criticizing
- Collective action: no close-up faces, no individual naming

**No reference image needed.** Context description is sufficient for Omni Flash to render correctly.

### Appearance Rhythm by Beat

| Beat | How Line C appears |
|---|---|
| **B1 — HOOK** | Absent / or 1 distant blurred silhouette — texture only |
| **B2 — SETUP 1** | Present unintentionally — unknowing witnesses |
| **B3–4 — ESCALATION** | Sparse — giving space to A↔B conflict |
| **B5 — ESCALATION 2** | Surges if there's a crowd / market / public event |
| **B6 — TWIST/CLIMAX** | **COMPLETELY ABSENT** — quiet space for the most important moment |
| **B7 — RESOLUTION** | 1-2 people passing by, not looking — life continues |
| **B8 — CATHARSIS** | Distant, blurred, bustling — life goes on, only A/B know what just happened |

### Line C with Dialogue — Greek Chorus Dialogue

> When Line C speaks: **camera DOES NOT cut to their face** — dialogue heard through shots of the protagonist.
> Audience hears the judgment but doesn't see who is speaking → Character A is judged without being allowed to defend → extremely powerful dramatic irony.

**Line C Dialogue Conditions:**
- ≤ 80 characters/line — short, passing like casual gossip
- Tone: whispered, calm, gossiping — NOT dramatic, NOT loud
- Maximum 2 Lines C dialogue in 1 clip (15 seconds)

**Example dialogue — correct technique:**

| Context | Line C says | Truth the audience knows |
|---|---|---|
| Father returns after 15 years | `"Empty hands. Typical."` | He gave up everything to not become a burden |
| Protagonist silently absorbs hurt | `"She should just kick him out."` | She's trying to keep the only person she has left |
| Antagonist smiling, generous | `"He's so generous. Nothing like his brother."` | That is a mask hiding calculation |
| Crowd flees in fear | `"Run — just run!"` | Protagonist cannot run — someone still needs to be saved |

## § PROPS & SYMBOLIC OBJECTS

- **Through-line symbolic object**: MANDATORY 1 object, appears ≥ 2 times, with State Chain per Phase
- Prop name: short English noun (Basket, Letter, Key, Photo, Watch...) — DO NOT embed description into the name
- Prop profile: material → shape/size → color/texture → weight/how held → Initial State → State Chain per Phase
- **"the only [prop name] in frame"** — always add to prevent duplication

## § SHOW, DON'T TELL

- Emotions, motivations, lessons ALL must be **encoded into specific actions / objects / light**
- NO captions, explanatory text overlaid on image
- **"Mirror shot" technique**: AFTERMATH phase reverses images from HOOK/SETUP — audience recognizes the story's full circle through image, without words

## § CROSS-SCENE CONTINUITY

**HANDOFF STATE (Final State Gate)** — mandatory at end of every Scene, covering:
- Character: [camera-anchored posture, frame-left/right position, expression]
- Prop: [position, damage state, holder]
- Setting: [lighting, time of day, weather]

### Unified Space-Time Architecture:
1. **Single Floor Plane Lock**: 100% of interacting characters in a 10s Scene MUST be on the same floor plane, 1.5m–3m apart. Anchor immediately with camera-relative coords: `Lok at frame-left; Keth at frame-right on the same floor, 2 meters apart`.
2. **Subject-Centric Action Spec**: All scene description MUST follow: *(1) Primary movement ➔ (2) Interactions with environment/subjects ➔ (3) Emotional expressions & subtle gestures*.
3. **Prop Spatial Handoff**: FORBIDDEN for props to jump positions. Every position change MUST have a behavioral bridge: *Old position ➔ Pickup/place/transfer action ➔ New position*.
4. **Biomechanical Velocity Budget**: Maximum movement speed 1–1.5m/s (3–5 steps in 1 beat of 3–4s).
5. **Terminal Deceleration Protocol**: Last 0.5s of scene (seconds 09–10) MUST decelerate to zero velocity, locking into a static pose.
6. **Delta-Only Rule**: Script table only records state changes (Delta), not rewriting fixed descriptions in subsequent scenes.
7. **Causal Reaction Rule**: Within 1 beat, one person's action MUST be the direct causal reaction to the other person's action. Hands do not touch directly — use **Prop Intermediary**.
8. **Eyeline Match**: Explicitly specify gaze direction when 3+ people are in the same frame.

## § RESOLUTION BANK — ENDING TYPES

> Resolution is the **natural consequence** from the character arc + chosen conflict — NOT a random event from outside. Add to metadata: `RESOLUTION TYPE: [#N] — [name]`.

| # | Resolution Type | Application Condition | Target Emotion |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Triumph (Clear Victory)** | Character overcomes conflict through growth/right action | Excited, satisfied |
| R2 | **Pyrrhic Victory (Win but Lose)** | Conflict resolved but lost something important to do it | Bittersweet, lingering |
| R3 | **Tragic Downfall** | Complete Negative Arc — character doesn't change and faces full consequences | Haunting, heavy |
| R4 | **Redemption** | Character who did wrong now does right — even if late — to repair | Moved, hopeful |
| R5 | **Open Ending** | Conflict not fully resolved, but character has changed enough to continue | Curious, wanting more |
| R6 | **Ironic Reversal** | Character gets what they wanted but in a completely unexpected way — usually a consequence of their own choices | Shocked + reflective |
| R7 | **Sacrifice (Meaningful)** | Character gives up what matters most to protect others / a greater value | Deeply moved, respectful |
| R8 | **Status Quo Restored + Changed** | World returns to normal but character has changed irreversibly | Matured, quietly profound |

**Resolution Rules:**
- Use **the exact symbolic prop planted in SETUP** to visualize the resolution — do not introduce a new object
- Resolution is the natural consequence of the character's own choices/actions — not divine intervention or random luck
- After Resolution: Aftermath is STILL, slow, with mirror shot — NO shouting/exaggeration

## § NARRATIVE STRUCTURE — 7 TYPES

> Default is Structure A. AI is allowed and encouraged to randomly select B-G, prioritizing non-repetition of recently used structures.

| # | Structure | Arrangement | When it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Standard Linear | Hook → Setup → Escalation → Twist → Resolution+Aftermath | Safe, clear, works for all genres |
| **B** | Mirror Loop | Opens with image of character in initial state → Setup → Escalation → Twist → Resolution, then CLOSES with exact same opening image but position/state reversed | Character transforms deeply, complete circle |
| **C** | Consequence First | Opens with the actual Resolution/consequence moment (reason still unclear) → flashes back to Setup/Escalation → Twist is the "decoder" → Aftermath closes | Strong "reverse curiosity", works for very short video (3-4 scenes) |
| **D** | Dual Parallel | Intercuts between Character A and Character B both moving toward a convergence point, meeting exactly at the Twist | Heightens drama (intercut), 2 equally important characters |
| **E** | Witness Narrator | Supporting character at Hook as observer, main storyline through this person's viewpoint/memory, Resolution+Aftermath returns to the witness's frame | Creates natural "framing device" layer |
| **F** | Cause Across Time | Compress Setup+Escalation at "earlier phase" very briefly, Twist is the transition to "later phase", Resolution+Aftermath in later phase repeats the motif | Story with temporal depth, multiple generations |
| **G** | Single Moment Compression | Hook and Setup merge into one extremely short scene, entire story within one time/space unit, leads directly to Twist then Resolution | Works for extremely short video (3 scenes, ~30s) |

**Mandatory rules when using B-G:**
- Twist must still have an insert-shot regardless of where it's positioned
- Resolution is still the natural consequence of the character's action/choice
- Clearly state in output: `NARRATIVE STRUCTURE: [A-G] + 1 explanatory sentence`

## § TWIST BANK — 12 CINEMATIC TYPES

#### 🎬 UNIVERSAL TWIST DESIGN FORMULA (3 STEPS)
1. **Step 1: Setup (Plant Belief)** [Phase 1-2]: Plant the situation causing misunderstanding or create a visual question in the first 3s (*Cognitive Gap*).
2. **Step 2: Misdirect (Lead Astray)** [Phase 3]: Reinforce the misunderstanding through action/detail, hide the real motive/secret.
3. **Step 3: Payoff (Reveal & Emotional Impact)** [Phase 4]: Unveil the truth through **symbolic prop insert-shot** + reveal of true identity/motive.

| # | Twist Type | Description & Reversal Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Consequence Twist** | Character's action causes the opposite of what they wanted — their own trap springs on them |
| 2 | **Identity Twist** | True identity revealed — person thought to be X turns out to be Y |
| 3 | **Perspective Twist** | Shift viewpoint — replay/reveal true motive behind a misunderstood action |
| 4 | **Reverse Role Twist** | Person thought to be strong/saving — is actually being saved/dependent on the other |
| 5 | **Hidden Kindness Twist** | Person who was hated/driven away was actually silently sacrificing to protect |
| 6 | **Symbolic Twist** | Symbolic prop insert-shot (*Chekhov's Gun*) reveals hidden meaning planted from the start |
| 7 | **Full Circle Twist** | Ending repeats the opening image but carries completely new meaning |
| 8 | **Moral Awakening** | Character collapses/awakens to realize their mistake when it's too late |
| 9 | **Redemption Twist** | Character who did something bad now performs a surprising good action at the last moment |
| 10 | **Forgiveness Twist** | Forgiveness instead of revenge — moral direction bends against expectations |
| 11 | **Redirect Twist** | Change the direction of outcome — what was expected to go to A instead goes to B |
| 12 | **Secret Connection** | Two people/events are connected to each other from before — neither (nor the audience) knew |
