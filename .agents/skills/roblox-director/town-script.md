# Roblox "In this town" scripts

This is how to write viral Roblox story shorts. It is built from:
- 6 viral Instagram/TikTok reels, measured line by line (bell, memories, town, dog, monster, train, plus 2026-11-15 refs like "You have to do whatever Simon says" and "In this town, I'd cry every day")
- the YouTube breakdown "How I make viral Roblox stories with Claude" (That Guy Animation, youtu.be/YhjjHh6Y2xc)

If the `roblox` skill is installed too, the full detail and the checker tools are in its folder (`script.md`, `tools/script_check.py`, `tools/plan_timing.py`, `templates/script-sheet.md`). For world-rule storytime this skill wins where they disagree on story shape; `script.md` wins on numbers (timing, sentence lengths). Every other Roblox genre (drama, mystery, series without a world rule) takes its structure from `story-structure.md` and its voice/numbers from `script.md` (see the precedence note at the top of `story-structure.md`).

## 1. The rule (the whole video lives or dies here)
**Formula:** `In this town, [an everyday thing] [does one strange thing] [when / for whom].`
Also allowed: "In this world," or a rule said as an order ("You have to do whatever Simon says.").

The rule must pass all four tests:
1. **You can SEE it.** It shows on a person or in the street: a kid shrinks when he lies, a front door changes colour when you hide something, a bridge only appears for people telling the truth, a number floats over every head. If it needs explaining, it's the wrong rule.
2. **Clear in 3 seconds with the sound off.** One sentence, 8–15 words, spoken first (0.0–0.2 s). It is also written big on screen in the first second.
3. **It costs the narrator something every day.**
4. **It can flip** (cost → gift, gift → trap), so a twist is possible.

The rule is in present tense; the story is in past tense. Sentence 2 or 3 adds a limit (never, only, can't, one day) within about 12 s.

**Every idea is a rule + its twist + its last line, never a rule alone.** The twist is designed together with the rule: the rule is the promise, and the twist is the hidden way the rule really works (or who it really works on). An idea without a twist is not an idea yet.

**Be creative, not generic:**
- A strange, specific everyday object: a front door, an ice cream truck, an umbrella, a town clock, a shadow's colour, red strings on fingers. Not "a mark", not "powers".
- An emotional engine inside the family: a parent who lies to protect you, a grandparent paying a hidden price, a promise to Dad.
- The twist flips how the viewer read the rule (it counts down to *saving* someone, not your best day) or whom it was about (the truck stopped for Mom, not me).
- The last line moves the danger somewhere new: a new number on the sister, a string pulling toward the lake.
- Mix tones across the 10: creepy, sad, warm, funny. Never two ideas with the same twist family.

**Picking a rule:** write 10 visible ideas. Keep the one that makes you say "wait, what happens then?" and whose twist makes you want to rewatch. Stay out of the crowded lanes ("X is deadly", "everyone gets X", plain monster rules) unless the object is new. When the user gives a rule, keep their line and only tighten the wording.

## 1b. The creativity engine (use it every time you make ideas)
Lesson learned: "the scripts are not creative." The failure was 9 of 10 ideas being the same kind of rule: an object that DETECTS a hidden feeling (rings at lies, floats at truth, stops when someone waits). Viral refs are never only that.

**Rule lanes.** Every batch of 10 uses at least 6 lanes, and no lane more than twice. Detector is max 1 in 10.
1. **Market:** the thing can be bought, sold or swapped. Ref method: "you can sell your memories". Ask: who gets rich, who gets poor?
2. **Town event:** a test, ceremony or day everyone must go through. Ref: the test at 18, picture day, Moving Day.
3. **Broken world:** physics or nature stuck or flipped. Ref: the train that never stops, the sun afraid of the dark, gravity off on Sundays.
4. **Living giant:** a huge thing is a person with a small, childish feeling (the Sun is scared, the Moon is lonely, the town clock is lazy, the volcano has hiccups).
5. **Body:** something visible on every body changes (height, a number, glowing hands, a shadow pet).
6. **Law said as an order:** "You have to do whatever Simon says." "Nobody can say the word 'no'."
7. **Limit:** everyone gets only N of something (words a day, 3 doors, one color).
8. **Swap:** two things trade places every day (kids and parents, cats and dogs, day jobs).
9. **Detector:** an object reveals a hidden feeling. Max 1 per batch.

**Lenses** (run each rule through at least 2 before keeping it):
- **Invert:** give the most powerful thing the weakest feeling, or the weakest thing a giant power.
- **Make it a system:** what shop, job, law, school class or TV show exists because of the rule? Show one in the first 20 %.
- **Price tag:** what does it cost the kid every single day, in one picture?
- **Kid logic:** how would a 9-year-old cheat it, and what goes wrong?
- **Debate hook:** the rule makes viewers argue in the comments ("would you sell yours?", "I'd pick the cat"). Write the comment question.
- **Collide two worlds:** mix two everyday places (school + weather, bakery + memories, bus + time).

**Twists that reframe, not reassign.** A strong twist changes what the WHOLE rule means (the market was buying something else, the test was testing the parents, the sun's fear is what keeps night safe). "It was about someone else" (wrong target) is allowed once per batch at most.

**How to make 10:** write 30 one-line seeds fast (at least 3 per lane), pick 15 that pass the 4 rule tests, run 2 lenses on each, keep the 10 that score highest on: visible in 3 s, the system around it, twist strength, comment-debate, and how easy it is to film in Roblox (free assets: school, house, street, shop, nature). Mix tones: at least 2 funny, 2 creepy, 2 sad or warm, 1 big-spectacle.

**Banned patterns** (they read as generic AI ideas): "X glows / rings / changes colour when you lie", "when someone thinks of you", "it was a dream", "the narrator was dead all along", a rule only one family notices, a twist with no clue before it.

## 2. The shape (beats as % of runtime)
| beat | where | what |
|---|---|---|
| Rule | 0–6 % | first line = the rule; fully clear by 9 % |
| Me | by 6 s | first "I / my" line: why the rule matters to the narrator |
| Life under the rule | 8–25 % | 1–3 quick examples, at least one funny or with a price; the town cheers or accepts it |
| Grandparent's story | 15–30 % | one old memory about the rule (planted detail #3) |
| False belief | 10–36 % | everyone believes something about the rule; a person or sign says it, never "Everyone thought…" |
| The kid notices | 30–45 % | the narrator sees one thing that doesn't fit |
| Rule turns cruel | 40–55 % | something arrives that makes the rule dangerous |
| Twist line | 49–78 % | the truth; it turns the first line around |
| One clever physical action | 60–90 % | the narrator does one thing with his hands, using the rule (not a speech) |
| Payoff | 85–95 % | all 3 planted details pay off |
| Last line | ≥92 % | **the next danger**: a cliffhanger, never a moral |

**Plant 3 small details early, all of which pay off later:**
1. something the kid has to repeat (a habit, a phrase, a count)
2. something odd the kid noticed
3. something a grandparent once said

**Twist families** (rotate them; never the same as the last episode):
- hidden sacrifice (max 1 in 5 episodes, never for a sick or dying relative)
- narrator was the cause
- role flip
- rule misread
- the rule was a lie
- the outsider was right
- the rule becomes a reward
- the cost lands on the narrator
- a time reveal
- wrong target
- mirror

The twist must be fair: at least 2 clues the viewer saw before it.

**Endings:** concrete consequence → an image or short quote → a last line that opens the next danger ("…and tonight, my door turned red."). Never end on "and that's when I learned", a stated moral, or an explanation of the twist. People share cliffhangers, not lessons.

## 3. Length and words
| runtime | spoken words (about 220 wpm) | content |
|---|---|---|
| 20 s | ~65 | rule + one example + twist + cliffhanger |
| 60 s | ~200 | 1 example, at most 2 named characters |
| ~150 s (best for watch time) | ~530 | full shape, a proof scene in the middle |

Read at 200–240 wpm. Exactly 2–3 designed pauses (0.6–1.3 s): one after the twist line, one before the last line. No other gap of 0.5 s or more.

## 4. Voice (non-negotiable)
- **First person, one kid narrator**, telling a friend what happened. Very simple English (A1–A2): about 7 words per sentence, half the sentences 6 words or fewer, never more than 20.
- **Sounds human, not AI:** contractions, a casual word about every 15 s ("like", "honestly", "so yeah"), feelings shown as actions ("He put salt in his coffee."), never named ("I felt guilty").
- Every short fragment adds a new fact (a number, name, object, sound or place). No emphasis-only fragments ("Forever." "Not once.").
- **Banned:** "for the first time", "I finally understood / knew", "Everyone thought…", "That's when…", "and that's when I learned", aphorisms, movie lines, polished similes, "never let go".
- Never "Not X but Y" contrasts or one-line dramatic closers. Run the humanizer skill on every draft.
- **Safe for a young audience:** no gore, deaths happen off screen, adult threats are uncanny and never predatory, no swearing.
- **Never copy** a ref's story, lines or characters. Copy the method only.

## 5. Built to be filmed
- **Every line can be shown in Roblox:** a prop, a sign, a face, an action. Write it so there's about one shot per line.
- **The rule object** is on screen in shot 1 and in at least half the shots. Every change in its state is shown.
- **Readable props carry facts:** a glowing "8:59" clock, a nameplate, a chalkboard, a door colour. Put text in the top third of the frame.
- **Mark** `(0.6x)` on 1–3 short punch lines and the twist row as `TWIST`.

## 6. Output format (what the user gets)
1. **Idea shortlist (the outline):** a table of 10 ideas, `# | lane | In this town… | What you see (incl. the system: shop / job / law) | The twist (+ twist family) | Last line (next danger) | Comment question`, with the top 3 and why. Check the lane quota (6+ lanes, detector max 1, wrong-target max 1) before showing it.
2. **Script sheet:** a table with columns `# | line | beat | shot idea | on-screen text`.
   - tag beats: rule, me, example, grandpa, misread, notice, cruel, TWIST, action, payoff, cliffhanger
   - mark planted details P1/P2/P3 where they are set up and where they pay off
3. **Title:** the rule line itself, e.g. `In This Town, Your DOOR Changes Colour When You Lie 😳`.
4. **Checks (all must pass):**
   - rule visible and clear in 3 s
   - first "me" line by 6 s
   - twist at 49–78 %
   - 3 plants paid off
   - last line is a next danger
   - no banned phrase
   - word count fits the runtime
   - 3 logic questions a commenter would ask ("why doesn't X just…?"), each answered in the script

**Stop after the script.** The user reads and edits it before any voice credits are spent or anything is built. Then hand off to the `roblox` skill for voice, build and edit.
